Part F — Passage-Based Questions
Read the passage carefully:

During the nineteenth century, improvements in transport and communication transformed the world economy. Railways connected agricultural regions with ports, while steamships carried goods across oceans more quickly. The development of refrigerated ships made it possible to transport perishable foods such as meat over long distances. These changes reduced transportation costs and helped connect producers and consumers in distant parts of the world. As a result, food and raw materials could increasingly be traded on a global scale.

Read the passage carefully:

Indentured labour migration was an important part of the nineteenth-century global economy. Workers from India and other parts of Asia were taken to plantations, mines and construction projects in different colonies. They signed contracts promising to work for a fixed period. Although the system was legally different from slavery, workers often experienced harsh conditions, low wages and restrictions on their freedom. Over time, indentured workers contributed to the cultural and social development of the societies in which they settled.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
The global world refers to increasing connections between different regions through trade, migration, technology, investment and cultural exchange.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
The potato was brought from the Americas to Europe and became an important food crop, particularly for poorer Europeans.
Q3. Correct Answer: B
Poverty, unemployment, population growth and food shortages encouraged many Europeans to migrate overseas in search of better opportunities.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
The Corn Laws imposed restrictions on the import of food grains into Britain. Their abolition helped open Britain to cheaper food imports.
Q5. Correct Answer: C
After the Corn Laws were abolished, Britain could import food more cheaply. This encouraged global agricultural trade.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Railways and steamships greatly reduced transportation time and costs and connected producers with distant markets.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Refrigerated ships made it possible to carry perishable foods such as meat over long distances without rapid spoilage.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Indentured labour involved workers signing contracts to work for a specified period, often under difficult conditions.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Caribbean colonies were among the important destinations for Indian indentured workers employed mainly on plantations.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Fiji was one of the important destinations for Indian indentured labourers.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
The abolition of the Corn Laws came before the First World War, followed by the Great Depression and then the Second World War.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
The nineteenth-century global economy developed before the First World War, followed by the Great Depression and the Second World War.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Corn Laws = restrictions on food imports; indentured labour = contract labour; refrigerated ships = perishable food transport; Silk Routes = long-distance trade and cultural connections.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
India supplied indentured labour, Caribbean colonies received many workers, Europe experienced large migration and America became a major destination for European migrants.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Trade, migration and technological improvements helped connect different parts of the world and created a more integrated world economy.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
After the Corn Laws were abolished, Britain could import cheaper food from countries with abundant land and agricultural production.
Q17. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false. Indentured labour was not completely free from exploitation. The reason is true because workers could face harsh conditions and restrictions.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Railways and steamships reduced transportation time and costs, directly contributing to the expansion of international trade.
Q19. Correct Answer: B
The global economy developed through several interconnected processes, including trade, migration, capital flows and technological change.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Migration helped relieve population pressure in some European countries and supplied labour to developing economies overseas.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Refrigerated ships were especially important because they enabled perishable food such as meat to be transported across oceans.
Q22. Correct Answer: B
Poverty, indebtedness, loss of land and the promise of employment encouraged many Indians to enter the indentured labour system.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Colonial economies became increasingly connected to international markets and were often reorganised to supply raw materials and agricultural products.
Q24. Correct Answer: B
Railways connected inland agricultural areas with ports and markets, making the movement of agricultural goods easier.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Refrigerated ships allowed meat and other perishable foods to travel long distances without spoiling quickly.
Q26. Correct Answer: B
The passage describes how transportation technology helped integrate distant producers and consumers into a global economy.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Indentured workers signed contracts that generally required them to work for a fixed period.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Indentured migration involved contractual obligations and could involve harsh conditions and restrictions on workers' freedom.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Indian migrants carried languages, religions, food, customs and traditions to different parts of the world, contributing to new multicultural societies.
Q30. Correct Answer: B
The making of the global world was a long historical process shaped by trade, migration, technology, capital and changing economic relationships.
