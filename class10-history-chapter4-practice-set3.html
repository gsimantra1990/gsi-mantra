Part F — Passage-Based Questions
Read the passage carefully:

Before the rise of large factories, merchants often organised production through rural households. They supplied raw materials to peasants and artisans and collected finished goods later. This system allowed merchants to expand production without being restricted by urban guilds. It also provided employment to rural families, who combined agricultural work with textile and other manufacturing activities.

Q25. What system of production is described in the passage?

Q26. Why did merchants organise production in rural areas?

Q27. What advantage did rural families receive from this system?

Read the passage carefully:

During the First World War, British industries were increasingly occupied with producing goods for the war. As a result, imports of manufactured goods into India declined. Indian factories were asked to supply goods for the war and therefore increased their production. This created new opportunities for Indian industries and expanded the demand for industrial labour.

Q28. Why did imports into India decline during the First World War?

Q29. What was one effect of the war on Indian factories?

Q30. Which broader conclusion can be drawn from the passage?

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Proto-industrialisation involved large-scale production organised through merchants and rural households before the widespread development of factories.
Q2. Correct Answer: B
Merchants shifted production to the countryside because urban guilds imposed restrictions on production and entry into trades.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
The Spinning Jenny increased the number of threads that could be spun by one worker and improved spinning productivity.
Q4. Correct Answer: B
James Hargreaves is credited with inventing the Spinning Jenny in the eighteenth century.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
The steam engine provided mechanical power and became important in several industrial processes.
Q6. Correct Answer: B
Hand labour remained useful because many specialised and delicate products required human skill and careful finishing.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Some industries had seasonal demand, so employers needed workers only during particular periods of the year.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Cotton textiles were one of the leading sectors of Britain's early industrialisation.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Iron and steel are associated with heavy industries and played an important role in industrial development.
Q10. Correct Answer: B
The East India Company established political control and used agents to regulate weavers and the textile trade.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
The Spinning Jenny came first, followed by improvements to the steam engine, the development of cotton mills in Bombay, and finally the First World War.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation began in Britain before the establishment of the first cotton mill in Bombay, followed by the Swadeshi movement and then the First World War.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
The decline of some exports preceded the establishment and growth of Indian mills, while wartime demand later expanded production.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
James Hargreaves — Spinning Jenny; James Watt — steam engine improvements; Eli Whitney — cotton gin; John Kay — Flying Shuttle.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Proto-industrialisation was production before factories became widespread; jobbers recruited workers; gomasthas supervised weavers; the Spinning Jenny increased spinning capacity.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
These industrialists were associated with different stages and areas of Indian industrial development.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
Both statements are true. Hand labour was particularly useful for products requiring detailed work and specialised skills.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Indian handloom production survived partly because weavers adapted to changing markets and specialised in certain types of cloth.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
The First World War reduced imports because British industries were occupied with war production, creating opportunities for Indian industries.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Manufacturers used advertisements to attract consumers and create demand for their products.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Urban guilds controlled many trades and restricted entry, making it difficult for merchants to expand production within towns.
Q22. Correct Answer: B
Labour could be relatively cheap, while machines were not suitable for every type of product. Therefore, industrialists often used both machines and workers.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Machine-made British textiles created strong competition for Indian textile producers in several markets.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
Advertisements helped industrialists create product identities, attract consumers and increase demand.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
The passage describes proto-industrial production, where merchants organised production through rural households before factories became widespread.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Rural production helped merchants obtain labour and avoid the restrictions imposed by urban guilds.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Rural families could combine agricultural work with manufacturing, providing an additional source of income.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
British factories were increasingly occupied with wartime production, reducing their ability to supply ordinary manufactured goods to India.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Indian factories increased production to meet wartime requirements when imports declined.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
The First World War affected international trade and production patterns and created temporary opportunities for Indian industries.
