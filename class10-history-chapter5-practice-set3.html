Part E — Passage Based Questions
Passage:

The spread of print created new forms of communication and debate. Religious thinkers, reformers and political writers could reach audiences beyond their immediate local communities. At the same time, authorities often viewed certain printed texts with suspicion. The circulation of ideas therefore produced both opportunities for discussion and attempts to control information.

Q21. What central transformation is highlighted in the passage?

Q23. Which inference can be drawn from the passage?

Passage:

In nineteenth-century India, newspapers and journals became important means of discussing social and political questions. Reformers used print to criticize harmful practices and promote new ideas. At the same time, orthodox groups also used print to defend traditional practices. Thus, print became a field in which different groups contested ideas and attempted to influence public opinion.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Print culture became influential because printed texts could be reproduced in large numbers and circulated among increasingly wider reading communities.
Q2. Correct Answer: B
The Catholic Church used the Index of Prohibited Books to identify works considered harmful or contrary to Catholic teachings.
Q3. Correct Answer: B
Printing helped Protestant ideas travel rapidly across different regions, making religious debates much wider than before.
Q4. Correct Answer: B
Luther's writings were printed and widely circulated, helping spread criticism of established Catholic practices.
Q5. Correct Answer: B
The print revolution affected society by changing how knowledge, religious ideas, political arguments and criticism circulated.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Printed pamphlets, newspapers and other materials helped circulate political ideas and criticism during the revolutionary period.
Q7. Correct Answer: B
Almanacs were among the forms of popular print that reached ordinary readers and contained practical information.
Q8. Correct Answer: B
Increasing literacy combined with cheaper printed material expanded the potential reading public.
Q9. Correct Answer: B
Vernacular printing made literature and ideas available to people who did not read classical languages such as Latin.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Printed newspapers, journals and books became important platforms for debates about caste, religion and social reform.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
Print allowed ideas to cross geographical boundaries, creating new opportunities for public discussion and debate.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Print could encourage debate, but authorities could also use censorship and regulation to control particular publications.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers and journals helped circulate political ideas and provided spaces where colonial policies and nationalism could be discussed.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Expansion of women's education increased the number of women who could participate as readers and, in some cases, as writers.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Colonial authorities were concerned about publications that criticized colonial rule or helped spread nationalist political awareness.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
Gutenberg is associated with the European printing press; Luther with religious reform writings; Rammohun Roy with social and religious reform; and Rashsundari Devi with women's autobiographical writing.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
The print revolution transformed knowledge circulation, vernacular presses expanded regional-language publishing, cheap literature reached wider audiences, and print created new opportunities for women readers and writers.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
The development of printing enabled wider circulation of texts, which contributed to religious debates and eventually to the expansion of reading communities.
Q19. Correct Answer: B
Lower production costs and increasing literacy were important factors in the expansion of the reading public.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Print could expand debate while simultaneously provoking attempts by religious and political authorities to regulate information.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
The passage emphasizes that print allowed ideas and debates to reach audiences beyond immediate local communities.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Authorities could view printed criticism and alternative ideas as potentially challenging established religious or political authority.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
The passage demonstrates that the circulation of information created opportunities for debate as well as attempts to control information.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
Print provided space for both reformers and defenders of traditional practices to present their arguments.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Print culture became a field of debate in which different groups could challenge, defend or reinterpret existing social and religious ideas.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Printed texts could be read aloud or discussed orally, allowing print and oral traditions to interact rather than one immediately replacing the other.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
The colonial government sometimes attempted to regulate the press because newspapers could criticize colonial policies and contribute to political awareness.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
As literacy expanded, more people became potential readers, increasing the social reach of printed material.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Print created channels through which information, criticism and political or social ideas could reach larger groups and contribute to public discussion.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Print culture transformed the circulation of knowledge and played an important role in religious debates, social reform, political discussion and the development of modern public opinion.
