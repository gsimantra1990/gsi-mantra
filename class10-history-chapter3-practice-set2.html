Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: A
The Silk Routes were ancient networks of trade routes connecting Asia with Europe and facilitating the exchange of goods, ideas and cultures.
Q2. Correct Answer: B
Smallpox was carried to the Americas by European conquerors and settlers. Indigenous populations had little immunity to the disease, causing devastating losses.
Q3. Correct Answer: B
The Americas offered European powers valuable land, natural resources, agricultural products and new markets.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
The potato came from the Americas and became an important food crop in Europe.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
Population growth, unemployment, poverty and limited economic opportunities encouraged many Europeans to migrate overseas.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
The abolition of the Corn Laws allowed Britain to import food more freely and cheaply from other parts of the world.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Steamships were faster and more reliable than older sailing vessels and contributed greatly to the expansion of global trade.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Refrigeration made it possible to preserve perishable food, particularly meat, during long-distance transportation.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Many Indian peasants and labourers entered the indentured labour system because of poverty, indebtedness and the promise of employment.
Q10. Correct Answer: D
Fiji, Trinidad and Guyana were destinations for Indian indentured workers. Switzerland was not a major destination of this system.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
Globalisation broadly refers to increasing connections and interdependence among different regions of the world.
Q12. Correct Answer: B
After the Corn Laws were abolished, Britain could import cheaper food from countries where food production costs were lower.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Railways connected inland agricultural areas with ports and markets and made the movement of goods faster and cheaper.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Many European migrants moved to America in search of employment, land and better economic opportunities.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Indentured workers signed contracts requiring them to work for a specified period.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
The expansion of world trade was closely connected with improvements in transportation that reduced the cost and time of moving goods.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
Refrigerated ships allowed perishable foods such as meat to be transported over long distances, directly supporting the growth of food trade.
Q18. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false because indentured labour was legally different from slavery. The reason is true because indentured workers generally worked under fixed-term contracts.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
Migration helped create new communities because migrants carried their languages, food, customs and cultural practices to new places.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Silk Routes were associated with long-distance trade and cultural exchange. Corn Laws restricted food imports, refrigerated ships transported perishable foods and indentured labour involved contractual work.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Fiji, Trinidad and Guyana were among the destinations of Indian indentured workers, while India was an important source of indentured labour.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
The abolition of the Corn Laws occurred in the nineteenth century, before the First World War, the Great Depression and the Second World War.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Technological developments supported greater movement of goods and people and contributed to the expansion of global markets.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
The passage specifically mentions railways as one of the technologies that connected different regions.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
The telegraph greatly increased the speed at which information could travel across long distances.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Improved transport and communication helped producers reach distant markets and encouraged international trade.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Many Indian indentured workers came from villages and poorer sections of society.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Indentured workers were employed in plantations, mines and other labour-intensive activities in different colonies.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Although indentured workers worked under contracts, many experienced harsh working conditions, low wages and restrictions on their freedom.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
The making of the global world was a long historical process involving trade, migration, technology, capital flows and increasing connections among regions.
