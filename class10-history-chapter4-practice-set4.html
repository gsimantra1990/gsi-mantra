Part E — Passage-Based Questions
Read the passage carefully:

The spread of print created a new world of communication. Books, newspapers, pamphlets and journals could reach readers in different regions. Print did not simply increase the number of books; it also changed the way people exchanged ideas. Religious debates, social reform discussions and political arguments could be circulated more widely. At the same time, governments and religious authorities sometimes tried to control printed material because they feared the spread of ideas that challenged established authority.

Q24. What major change did print bring to communication?

Q26. Which forms of printed material are mentioned in the passage?

Read the passage carefully:

In nineteenth-century India, print became closely connected with social and religious reform. Reformers used newspapers and journals to discuss issues such as caste discrimination, women's education and religious practices. At the same time, conservative groups also used print to defend their views. Thus, print created a space in which different groups could express their arguments and respond to one another.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: A
China is associated with the early development of woodblock printing. Printed material was produced there centuries before the European printing revolution.
Q2. Correct Answer: B
Johannes Gutenberg developed the movable-type printing press in Europe in the fifteenth century.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
One of Gutenberg's most famous printed works was the Bible, produced using his printing technology.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
Printing made it possible to reproduce texts more quickly and in larger numbers than handwritten copying.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
Manuscripts were generally copied by hand. This required considerable time, skill and labour, making them relatively expensive.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Martin Luther used printed writings to spread his religious criticism and ideas during the Protestant Reformation.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Luther's Ninety-Five Theses criticized certain practices of the Catholic Church and became an important text of the Protestant Reformation.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
The growth of education and printing expanded the reading public, including educated middle-class groups.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
The Roman Catholic Church maintained an Index of Prohibited Books to identify publications considered unacceptable by Church authorities.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Increasing literacy together with the availability of printed books helped create a new reading culture in Europe.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
Gutenberg's press came first, followed by the wider spread of print culture in Europe. Printing later reached India and newspapers and journals expanded further.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
The development moved broadly from handwritten manuscripts to woodblock printing, movable type and eventually large-scale printing.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
The print revolution refers to the major transformation in how written texts were produced, distributed and consumed.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Some authorities feared that printed material could spread rebellious, critical or irreligious ideas among ordinary people.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Printed copies helped Luther's writings circulate widely, contributing to the spread of Reformation ideas.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
Printed books could be reproduced in larger quantities, allowing knowledge to reach many more readers.
Q17. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false because print did affect political ideas. The reason is true because newspapers and pamphlets circulated political discussions.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Print provided a means through which different viewpoints could circulate, encouraging debate and discussion.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
Portuguese missionaries introduced the printing press to Goa in the sixteenth century.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers, journals and pamphlets became important tools for discussing social and religious reform in nineteenth-century India.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers provided a platform for discussing political, social and economic issues and helped create public opinion.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Raja Rammohun Roy was an important social reformer who used print to discuss social and religious issues.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Colonial authorities feared criticism and the spread of nationalist ideas, which led to restrictions on sections of the Indian press.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
Print enabled written ideas to circulate among a much wider audience than was possible through many handwritten copies.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Authorities sometimes feared that printed writings could challenge religious, political or social authority.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
The passage specifically mentions books, newspapers, pamphlets and journals as forms of printed material.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Reformers used print to discuss social and religious problems and to communicate arguments supporting reform.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Print was not used only by reformers. Conservative groups also used newspapers and other printed materials to defend their positions.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Women's education was one of the social issues discussed through print during the nineteenth century.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Print expanded the circulation of ideas and created new spaces for discussion, debate, reform and political communication.
