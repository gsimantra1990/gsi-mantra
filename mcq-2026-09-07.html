Q1. How did the physical and material properties of early Chinese paper directly dictate the structural evolution of the traditional Chinese "accordion book"?

✅ Correct Answer: 3

Thin, porous Chinese paper was generally not suited to effective double-sided printing. Therefore, sheets were folded in an accordion-like manner and bound at the edge to create the traditional book structure.

Woodblock printing was the dominant technology in this phase. Metal movable type was not the reason for the development of the accordion format. Heavy parchment and leather-spine binding were not characteristic features of this traditional Chinese book form.

Q2. What was the primary socioeconomic and administrative catalyst behind the massive surge in the volume of printed texts in 16th-century China?

✅ Correct Answer: 1

The expansion of the civil service examination system created a huge demand for textbooks, reference works and examination-preparation materials.

The European mechanical press did not drive this 16th-century Chinese printing expansion. Printed and handwritten texts also continued to coexist.

Q3. How did the diversification of print culture in 17th-century China reflect a broader structural shift in urban society away from the rigid administrative monopoly of scholar-officials?

✅ Correct Answer: 4

17th-century urban prosperity expanded the market for printed material. Print was increasingly used for business and trade information as well as popular novels, drama and entertainment.

Thus, print culture moved beyond the narrow sphere of official administrative and scholarly texts and reached a much wider urban reading public.

Q4. What does the proliferation of published poetry, plays, and memoirs by elite women, officials' wives, and courtesans in 17th-century China reveal about the shifting boundaries of print culture?

✅ Correct Answer: 2

The growth of commercial urban printing created new literary opportunities. Elite women, officials' wives and courtesans could participate in literary culture through poetry, drama and memoirs.

This indicates that print culture was not limited exclusively to official male scholarship and religious literature.

Q5. Which combination of factors catalyzed the transition from traditional Chinese hand-printed woodblocks to Western-style mechanical printing presses in 19th-century Shanghai?

✅ Correct Answer: 3

In 19th-century Shanghai, Western presence and treaty-port institutions contributed to the introduction and expansion of mechanical printing.

The growth of Western-style education, newspapers and modern periodicals created a demand for faster and larger-scale printing. The transition was therefore connected with new educational, commercial and colonial influences rather than the extinction of traditional artisans or a shortage of pear-wood blocks.
