Part E — Passage Based Advanced Questions
Read the passage carefully:

The spread of print created new possibilities for debate and discussion. Reformers used newspapers, journals and books to challenge established practices. At the same time, authorities worried that printed criticism could encourage opposition. Thus, print became a field of both reform and control. Its impact depended not only on the technology of printing but also on literacy, readership, language and political conditions.

Read the passage carefully:

In colonial India, print became an important means through which social reformers, religious thinkers and political activists communicated with readers. Newspapers and journals discussed issues such as caste, women's education, religious reform and colonial policies. The expansion of vernacular print enabled ideas to circulate among readers who did not necessarily read English. Colonial authorities sometimes attempted to restrict such publications.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Gutenberg's movable metal type press made the reproduction of texts much faster and more efficient, helping print expand widely.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
Printing allowed Luther's writings and arguments to be reproduced in large numbers and circulated among readers.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
Authorities feared that readers might use printed texts to question established religious interpretations and institutions.
Q4. Correct Answer: B
Print increased the availability and affordability of texts and therefore helped expand the reading public.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
Print helped Protestant reformers circulate criticism and religious arguments, contributing to wider religious debate.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Because many people could not read, printed texts were often read aloud. Thus oral and print cultures interacted rather than one immediately replacing the other.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers and journals provided spaces for discussion of social, political and economic questions.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Colonial authorities were concerned that vernacular newspapers could spread criticism and political awareness among Indian readers.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Raja Rammohun Roy used newspapers and other forms of print to communicate reformist ideas.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Cheap print increased access to newspapers, stories, religious texts and other forms of popular literature.
Q11. Correct Answer: B
Even where literacy was limited, texts could be shared through collective reading and oral communication.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Attempts at censorship indicate that authorities recognised the capacity of print to influence public discussion and political opinion.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Print enabled different groups to circulate competing ideas, making it useful for reform but also capable of generating conflict.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers discussing colonial policies demonstrate how print could become part of political debate.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Print gave authors greater opportunities to circulate arguments and gave readers access to diverse viewpoints.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
The ability to reproduce and circulate arguments more rapidly directly contributed to wider intellectual debate.
Q17. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false. Oral traditions continued to exist, and printed material was often read aloud. The reason is true.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Colonial authorities regulated some publications because printed criticism could contribute to political awareness and opposition.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
Gutenberg is associated with the European printing press; Luther with religious reform writings; Rammohun Roy with social reform; and the Vernacular Press Act with newspaper regulation.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
The print revolution transformed text production; cheap books widened access; newspapers expanded mass communication; censorship attempted to control ideas.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
The social impact of print depended on literacy and the size and nature of its readership.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Print could support reform movements while simultaneously attracting attempts at censorship and control.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Language determined which groups could directly access and understand printed material.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
Vernacular print reached readers who did not necessarily have access to English-language publications.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Indian print culture addressed a wide range of social, religious and political questions.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Attempts to restrict publications demonstrate that authorities considered printed communication capable of influencing public debate.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
The impact of print was shaped by literacy, affordability, language and political restrictions. Therefore, access was not automatically equal.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers and journals helped create wider discussion of political issues and contributed to the circulation of nationalist ideas.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Print expanded access to ideas, but its social effects depended on wider social and political conditions.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Print was not merely a technological innovation. It transformed the circulation of knowledge, religious ideas, social reform arguments and political discussion.
