Read the passage carefully:

Before factories became an important part of industrial production, many merchants organised production through rural households. They supplied raw materials to peasants and artisans and collected the finished goods. This system allowed merchants to produce goods for wider markets while avoiding some restrictions imposed by urban guilds. It is often described as proto-industrialisation.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Proto-industrialisation involved production organised by merchants through rural households and small workshops before the widespread growth of factories.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
Urban guilds controlled entry into trades and production in many towns. Merchants therefore increasingly turned to the countryside.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
Merchants supplied raw materials to rural producers and collected the finished products for sale in wider markets.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
Cotton textiles became one of the major industries of industrial Britain and played a central role in industrialisation.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
James Hargreaves invented the Spinning Jenny, an important machine in the history of textile production.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
The Spinning Jenny enabled a worker to operate several spindles at the same time, increasing productivity.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
The steam engine provided mechanical power and became important in factories, transport and other industrial activities.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
James Watt improved the steam engine and made it much more useful for industrial purposes.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
The cotton gin helped separate cotton fibres from seeds, making cotton processing more efficient.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Cotton was a crucial raw material for the expanding British textile industry.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
Indian textile centres were well known for high-quality handwoven and hand-printed textiles.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
British machine-made textiles competed with Indian products, while colonial trade policies restricted Indian access to important foreign markets.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Indian textile exports to Britain faced duties and restrictions that made it difficult for Indian producers to compete.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Gomasthas were paid servants appointed by the East India Company to supervise weavers and collect supplies.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
The Company system often restricted weavers' freedom and required them to sell cloth under conditions controlled by Company officials.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
James Hargreaves — Spinning Jenny; James Watt — improved steam engine; Richard Arkwright — Water Frame; Samuel Crompton — Spinning Mule.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
Spinning Jenny was a spinning machine; the steam engine provided mechanical power; gomasthas supervised weavers; industrialists owned or managed industrial enterprises.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Factories did not immediately eliminate hand production because many specialised goods still required skilled manual work.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
The growth of factories created new wage-based employment because workers were needed to operate machines and perform industrial tasks.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Company control over supplies and sales placed many Indian weavers in a difficult economic position.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Merchants wanted greater control over production so that they could maintain regular supplies, manage quality and respond to market demand.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Hand labour remained useful for specialised products and for work requiring flexibility or detailed craftsmanship.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation created wage employment, but factory workers often faced long working hours and difficult conditions.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
Advertisements helped manufacturers attract consumers, create demand and give products a distinctive identity.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Proto-industrial production was largely carried out in rural households and small workshops.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Merchants could avoid some of the restrictions imposed by urban guilds by organising production in rural areas.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
British machine-made cotton goods increased competition for Indian textile producers in Indian markets.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Declining demand and increasing competition reduced the income opportunities of many traditional Indian weavers.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation did not immediately destroy hand production. Hand production continued alongside factories, especially for specialised and labour-intensive goods.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation was a broad process involving machines, new production systems, expanding markets and major changes in labour.
