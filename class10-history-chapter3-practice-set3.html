Part F — Passage-Based Questions
Read the passage carefully:

Before the nineteenth century, long-distance trade and cultural exchange had already connected different regions of the world. The Silk Routes were important examples of these connections. Traders carried silk, textiles, spices and other goods across long distances. Along with goods, travellers and merchants also carried ideas, religions and cultural practices. These networks show that global connections existed long before the modern period.

Q24. What did the Silk Routes mainly connect?

Q26. What else travelled along with goods?

Read the passage carefully:

The nineteenth-century global economy was closely connected with migration. Millions of Europeans migrated to different parts of the world. Many moved to America and Australia in search of land, employment and better economic opportunities. At the same time, large numbers of Indian and Chinese workers migrated as indentured labourers. These movements of people helped shape the economic and social life of many societies.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: A
The Silk Routes connected Asia with Europe and other regions through trade and cultural exchange.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
Smallpox was among the diseases that spread to indigenous populations in the Americas after European contact, causing devastating effects.
Q3. Correct Answer: B
The discovery and colonisation of the Americas created new trade routes and connected distant economies.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
The potato came from the Americas and became an important food for many Europeans, particularly the poor.
Q5. Correct Answer: B
The abolition of the Corn Laws allowed Britain to import food grains more freely and cheaply.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Steamships reduced the time and cost required to transport goods across oceans.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Refrigerated ships allowed perishable foods such as meat to be transported over long distances.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Fiji was one of the destinations where Indian indentured labourers were employed.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Many indentured workers came from poor peasant and working communities.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Poverty, indebtedness and the search for employment encouraged many Indians to enter the indentured labour system.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
The Corn Laws were abolished before the First World War, followed by the Great Depression and then the Second World War.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation helped expand world trade, while the First World War and Great Depression came later.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Silk Routes = ancient trade connections; potato = food from the Americas; Corn Laws = restrictions on food imports; refrigerated ships = transport of perishable food.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
India was a source of indentured labour, Caribbean economies relied heavily on plantation labour, Fiji received Indian workers and America attracted European migrants.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
The Silk Routes carried both goods and ideas, religions and cultural practices.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
Improved transport technologies such as railways and steamships directly helped expand world trade.
Q17. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false because indentured labour was not exactly the same as slavery. The reason is true because workers were bound by contracts for a fixed period.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Global economic changes affected ordinary people through migration, employment, food and trade.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
The Silk Routes connected different regions through both economic and cultural exchange.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
The potato became an important food crop and helped improve food availability for many Europeans.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Improved transportation and communication made international trade faster and more efficient.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Many Europeans migrated overseas because they wanted land, employment and better economic opportunities.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
The abolition of the Corn Laws enabled Britain to obtain food from overseas at lower prices.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
The Silk Routes connected different regions through long-distance trade and cultural interaction.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Silk, textiles, spices and other goods were traded through the networks known as the Silk Routes.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Ideas, religions and cultural practices travelled along with traders and goods.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Many European migrants went overseas in search of land, employment and better economic opportunities.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Indian and Chinese workers formed major groups involved in indentured labour migration.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Migration influenced the economic, social and cultural development of societies around the world.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
The making of the global world was a long historical process involving trade, migration, technological change and cultural exchange.
