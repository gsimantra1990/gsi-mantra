📚 History – Advanced MCQ Test

Nationalism in India • Harappan Civilization • Vedic Civilization

1. Which of the following was a major impact of the First World War on India?

Answer: B
The First World War increased defence expenditure and the colonial government imposed additional taxes, creating economic difficulties for Indians.
Exam Point: World War I created economic hardship, inflation and increased taxation in India.

2. During the First World War, forced recruitment of soldiers mainly affected which region?

Answer: B
Punjab experienced significant forced recruitment during the First World War.
Exam Point: Punjab was an important recruiting ground for the British Indian Army.

3. What was the basic idea behind Mahatma Gandhi's concept of Satyagraha?

Answer: B
Satyagraha was based on truth, non-violence and resistance against injustice.
Exam Point: Satyagraha literally emphasises the force or insistence of truth.

4. The Champaran Satyagraha was mainly related to which issue?

Answer: B
The Champaran movement was associated with the grievances of indigo cultivators, particularly the tinkathia arrangement.
Exam Point: Champaran Satyagraha – 1917 – Indigo cultivators.

5. What was the main cause of the Kheda Satyagraha?

Answer: C
The Kheda peasants sought relief from land revenue because crop failure had reduced their ability to pay.
Exam Point: Kheda Satyagraha – 1918 – Revenue relief.

6. The Ahmedabad Mill Strike was mainly associated with:

Answer: B
The Ahmedabad Mill Strike of 1918 involved textile workers and mill owners, with wages being a major issue.
Exam Point: Ahmedabad – 1918 – Mill workers.

7. What was one of the most controversial features of the Rowlatt Act?

Answer: B
The Rowlatt Act extended extraordinary powers to the colonial government, including detention without trial in certain circumstances.
Exam Point: Rowlatt Act – 1919 – popularly opposed as a repressive legislation.

8. The Non-Cooperation Movement was formally launched in:

Answer: C
The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched in 1920 and gained participation from various social groups.
Exam Point: Non-Cooperation Movement – 1920–22.

9. What was the immediate reason for the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement?

Answer: C
The violent incident at Chauri Chaura in February 1922 led Gandhi to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Exam Point: Chauri Chaura – 1922 – withdrawal of Non-Cooperation Movement.

10. Why was the Swaraj Party formed?

Answer: B
Swarajists wanted to enter legislative councils and obstruct colonial policies from within the constitutional framework.
Exam Point: C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru were important leaders of the Swaraj Party.

🏺 Harappan & Vedic Civilization

Ancient India • NCERT • Competitive Exam Oriented

11. The Harappan Civilization is also known as the:

Answer: B
The Harappan Civilization is commonly known as the Indus Valley Civilization, though the term Harappan Civilization is more geographically inclusive.
Exam Point: Harappan Civilization is named after Harappa, one of its major sites.

12. Which Harappan site is famous for the Great Bath?

Answer: B
The Great Bath is one of the most famous architectural structures discovered at Mohenjo-daro.
Exam Point: Great Bath → Mohenjo-daro.

13. Which Harappan site is famous for its ancient dockyard?

Answer: A
Lothal, located in present-day Gujarat, is famous for its structure traditionally identified as a dockyard.
Exam Point: Lothal → Gujarat → dockyard.

14. Which of the following was an important feature of Harappan cities?

Answer: A
Harappan urban centres are well known for planned streets, drainage systems and standardized construction.
Exam Point: Urban planning and drainage are major characteristics of Harappan cities.

15. The Harappan script is generally described as:

Answer: B
The Harappan script has not yet been conclusively deciphered.
Exam Point: Harappan script remains undeciphered.

16. Which crop was known to have been cultivated by the Harappans?

Answer: A
Evidence indicates that cotton was cultivated by the Harappans, making it an important early textile crop.
Exam Point: Cotton cultivation is an important feature of Harappan economy.

17. The earliest Veda is:

Answer: D
The Rigveda is generally regarded as the earliest of the four Vedas.
Exam Point: Rigveda → oldest Vedic text.

18. How many Vedas are traditionally recognised?

Answer: C
The four Vedas are Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda and Atharvaveda.
Exam Point: Rig + Sama + Yajur + Atharva = Four Vedas.

19. Which Veda is mainly associated with melodies and chants?

Answer: B
The Samaveda is particularly associated with melodies and chants used in Vedic rituals.
Exam Point: Sama → melodies and chants.

20. Which texts explain the meaning and significance of Vedic rituals?

Answer: A
The Brahmanas are prose texts that explain rituals and their significance in Vedic tradition.
Exam Point: Brahmanas → explanation of Vedic rituals.

21. Match the following:

Column I Column II
A. Champaran 1. Indigo cultivation
B. Kheda 2. Revenue relief
C. Ahmedabad 3. Mill workers
D. Rowlatt Satyagraha 4. Protest against repressive law


Codes:

A. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4

B. A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3

C. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2

D. A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1

Answer: A
Champaran was related to indigo cultivation; Kheda to revenue relief; Ahmedabad to mill workers; and Rowlatt Satyagraha to opposition to the Rowlatt Act.

22. Arrange the following events in chronological order:

1. Champaran Satyagraha

2. Kheda Satyagraha

3. Rowlatt Satyagraha

4. Non-Cooperation Movement


A. 1 → 2 → 3 → 4

B. 2 → 1 → 3 → 4

C. 3 → 1 → 2 → 4

D. 1 → 3 → 2 → 4

Answer: A
Champaran occurred in 1917, Kheda in 1918, Rowlatt Satyagraha in 1919 and Non-Cooperation began in 1920.

🌍 Geography – Advanced MCQ Test

Shaping of the Earth's Surface • NCERT Class 9 • Conceptual & Competitive

1. Which of the following best explains why the Earth's surface is continuously changing?

Answer: B
The Earth's surface is continuously modified by internal forces originating within the Earth and external forces acting on its surface.
Exam Point: Endogenic + Exogenic processes shape the Earth's surface.

2. Which of the following is an example of an endogenic force?

Answer: C
Volcanic activity originates from processes within the Earth and is therefore an endogenic process.

3. What is the fundamental difference between weathering and erosion?

Answer: B
Weathering breaks down rocks at their original location, whereas erosion includes removal and transportation of weathered material.
Exam Point: Weathering = breakdown in situ; Erosion = removal/transport.

4. Which process involves the breakdown of rocks without their transportation?

Answer: C
Weathering is the breakdown of rocks at or near the Earth's surface without transportation of the material.

5. Which of the following factors can contribute to weathering?

Answer: D
Physical, chemical and biological factors can contribute to the weathering of rocks.

6. Which agent is most directly associated with the formation of V-shaped valleys?

Answer: B
River erosion, particularly vertical erosion in the upper course, commonly creates V-shaped valleys.

7. In the upper course of a river, vertical erosion commonly results in the formation of:

Answer: C
Strong vertical erosion in the upper course of rivers produces deep V-shaped valleys.

8. A waterfall is most likely to develop when:

Answer: A
Differential erosion of hard and soft rock can produce a step or sudden drop in the river course, resulting in a waterfall.

9. Which landform is generally associated with the deposition of sediments at the mouth of a river?

Answer: B
A delta develops where a river loses energy and deposits sediments near its mouth.

10. Meanders are commonly associated with:

Answer: A
Meanders are pronounced bends commonly associated with the middle and lower courses of rivers.

11. Which process is mainly responsible for the formation of an ox-bow lake?

Answer: B
When a river cuts through the narrow neck of a meander, the abandoned loop can form an ox-bow lake.

12. Which of the following is a major erosional landform created by glaciers?

Answer: B
Glaciers erode valleys into broad U-shaped profiles.

13. Wind erosion is particularly effective in deserts because:

Answer: B
Sparse vegetation and abundant loose sediments allow wind to erode and transport material effectively in deserts.

14. Which of the following is primarily an erosional landform produced by sea waves?

Answer: A
Sea cliffs develop due to persistent erosion of coastal rocks by waves.

15. Sea waves can create caves, arches and stacks mainly through:

Answer: B
Wave erosion progressively enlarges weaknesses in coastal rocks, producing caves, arches and eventually stacks.

16. Match the following:

Column I Column II
A. River 1. U-shaped valley
B. Glacier 2. Sand dune
C. Wind 3. V-shaped valley
D. Sea waves 4. Sea cliff

A. A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4

B. A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2

C. A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3

D. A-4, B-2, C-3, D-1

Answer: A
Rivers commonly form V-shaped valleys, glaciers form U-shaped valleys, wind forms sand dunes and sea waves produce sea cliffs.

17. Arrange the following processes in the correct sequence:

1. Weathering

2. Erosion

3. Transportation

4. Deposition


A. 1 → 2 → 3 → 4

B. 2 → 1 → 4 → 3

C. 3 → 2 → 1 → 4

D. 4 → 3 → 2 → 1

Answer: A
A general sequence is weathering, erosion/removal, transportation and finally deposition.

18. Arrange the following river features according to their usual development from the upper course towards the lower course:

1. Delta

2. V-shaped valley

3. Meanders

4. Waterfall


A. 2 → 4 → 3 → 1

B. 4 → 2 → 3 → 1

C. 2 → 3 → 4 → 1

D. 1 → 3 → 4 → 2

Answer: A
V-shaped valleys and waterfalls are characteristic of upper-course processes, meanders are common downstream, and deltas form near the river mouth.
