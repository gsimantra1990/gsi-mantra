📘 Class 10 History – Nationalism in India

25 Advanced MCQs | NCERT Based | UPSC • BPSC • SSC • State PCS • CBSE

Q1. Which of the following was a major impact of the First World War on India?

Correct Answer: B

The First World War created a huge increase in defence expenditure. To meet this expenditure, the British government increased taxes and imposed additional economic burdens on the Indian people. Prices of essential goods also increased considerably during the war.

Q2. During the First World War, forced recruitment of soldiers mainly affected which region?

Correct Answer: B

During the First World War, the British government imposed forced recruitment in rural areas. Punjab was one of the regions where villagers faced considerable pressure to provide soldiers for the British army.

Q3. What was the basic idea behind Mahatma Gandhi's concept of Satyagraha?

Correct Answer: B

Satyagraha was based on the power of truth and non-violence. Gandhi believed that if the cause was just, people could resist oppression without using physical force. The aim was to appeal to the conscience of the oppressor.

Q4. According to Gandhi, what was an essential quality of a Satyagrahi?

Correct Answer: C

A Satyagrahi was expected to follow truth and non-violence. Gandhi considered moral courage more important than physical force. A Satyagrahi should peacefully resist injustice without hatred or violence.

Q5. The Champaran Satyagraha was mainly related to which issue?

Correct Answer: B

The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 was associated with indigo cultivation. Peasants were forced to cultivate indigo under the Tinkathia system. Gandhi intervened and supported the peasants against this exploitation.

Q6. Under which system were peasants in Champaran forced to cultivate indigo?

Correct Answer: B

Under the Tinkathia system, peasants in Champaran were compelled to cultivate indigo on a portion of their land. Gandhi's intervention helped expose the exploitative nature of this arrangement.

Q7. What was the main cause of the Kheda Satyagraha?

Correct Answer: C

The Kheda district of Gujarat suffered from crop failure and poor harvests. Despite this situation, the government demanded full land revenue. Gandhi and local leaders supported the peasants in their demand for revenue relief.

Q8. What was the principal demand of the peasants during the Kheda Satyagraha?

Correct Answer: B

The peasants of Kheda demanded suspension or relief from land revenue because crop failure had made it difficult for them to pay the government's demand.

Q9. The Ahmedabad Mill Strike was mainly associated with:

Correct Answer: B

The Ahmedabad Mill Strike of 1918 involved textile workers who demanded a higher wage. Gandhi supported the workers and used Satyagraha, including a hunger strike, to press for a settlement.

Q10. Which method did Gandhi use during the Ahmedabad Mill Strike?

Correct Answer: B

During the Ahmedabad Mill Strike, Gandhi undertook a hunger strike to support the workers and encourage them to remain firm in their demand. It was an example of his method of non-violent struggle.

Q11. What was one of the most controversial features of the Rowlatt Act?

Correct Answer: B

The Rowlatt Act gave the government extensive powers to repress political activities. Suspects could be arrested and detained without a normal trial. Gandhi strongly opposed this repressive law.

Q12. Gandhi launched which movement against the Rowlatt Act?

Correct Answer: B

Gandhi organised a nationwide Satyagraha against the Rowlatt Act in 1919. A hartal was organised on 6 April 1919 as part of the protest against the repressive legislation.

Q13. Which statement about the Rowlatt Satyagraha is correct?

Correct Answer: B

The Rowlatt Satyagraha was a mass protest against the Rowlatt Act. Hartals, demonstrations and peaceful resistance were important features of the movement.

Q14. The Non-Cooperation Movement was formally launched in:

Correct Answer: C

The Non-Cooperation Movement was adopted by the Congress in 1920. It was linked with the Khilafat issue and the demand for Swaraj. The movement encouraged people to withdraw cooperation from colonial institutions.

Q15. Which of the following was a programme of the Non-Cooperation Movement?

Correct Answer: A

The Non-Cooperation Movement encouraged people to boycott government schools, colleges, courts and other colonial institutions. The use of khadi and the boycott of foreign cloth were also important parts of the movement.

Q16. The meaning of the term 'Swaraj' was:

Correct Answer: B

Different social groups interpreted the idea of Swaraj in different ways. For some it meant freedom from colonial rule, while for peasants, workers, tribals and business groups it could also involve their specific social and economic demands.

Q17. What was the immediate reason for the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement?

Correct Answer: C

At Chauri Chaura in February 1922, a violent clash took place in which a police station was set on fire and policemen were killed. Gandhi believed that the movement had become violent and therefore withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement.

Q18. Why was the Swaraj Party formed?

Correct Answer: B

The Swaraj Party was formed in 1923 by leaders such as C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru. They wanted to enter the legislative councils and use them to obstruct colonial policies and expose the limitations of colonial government from within.

Q19. Who were among the important leaders associated with the Swaraj Party?

Correct Answer: A

C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru were the leading founders of the Swaraj Party in 1923. They believed that entering the legislative councils could be used as a political strategy against colonial rule.

Q20. What was the main strategy of the Swarajists?

Correct Answer: B

The Swarajists wanted to enter the legislative councils and use their presence to oppose colonial policies, disrupt government functioning and demonstrate that the councils were not truly representative institutions.

Q21. The Simon Commission came to India in:

Correct Answer: D

The Simon Commission arrived in India in 1928. It had been appointed by the British government to review constitutional arrangements, but its complete absence of Indian members led to widespread opposition.

Q22. Why was the Simon Commission widely opposed in India?

Correct Answer: A

The Simon Commission consisted entirely of British members. Indians strongly opposed the idea that a commission examining India's constitutional future should have no Indian representative. The famous slogan was "Simon Go Back".

Q23. Which slogan became famous during the protest against the Simon Commission?

Correct Answer: B

"Simon Go Back" became the popular slogan of the nationwide protests against the Simon Commission. Indians objected to the absence of any Indian member in the commission.

Q24. Arrange the following events in the correct chronological order:

1. Champaran Satyagraha
2. Kheda Satyagraha
3. Ahmedabad Mill Strike
4. Rowlatt Satyagraha

Correct Answer: A

The correct chronological order is: Champaran Satyagraha (1917) → Kheda Satyagraha (1918) → Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918) → Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919). This sequence is important for understanding the development of Gandhi's early movements in India.

Q25. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?

Correct Answer: B

Kheda Satyagraha was associated with the demand for revenue relief after crop failure. Champaran was associated with indigo cultivation, Ahmedabad with mill workers, and the Swaraj Party was formed later in 1923.

Jallianwala Bagh & Non-Cooperation Movement
📝 DAILY ADVANCED MCQ – 01 History
With reference to the events following the Jallianwala Bagh incident and the beginning of the Non-Cooperation-Khilafat Movement, consider the following statements:

1. General Dyer ordered firing at Jallianwala Bagh with the stated intention of creating terror and awe among the satyagrahis.

2. Mahatma Gandhi called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha after violence spread in different parts of the country.

3. Gandhi considered the Khilafat issue as an opportunity to bring Hindus and Muslims closer and build a broader-based national movement.

4. The Non-Cooperation programme adopted at Nagpur in December 1920 immediately began with a full-scale civil disobedience campaign.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A — 1, 2 and 3 only
Statement 1 — Correct:
General Dyer ordered firing on the crowd gathered at Jallianwala Bagh. He later stated that his objective was to produce a “moral effect” and create a feeling of terror and awe among the satyagrahis.

Statement 2 — Correct:
As violence spread following the Jallianwala Bagh incident and the government responded with brutal repression, Mahatma Gandhi called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha.

Statement 3 — Correct:
Gandhi wanted to launch a more broad-based national movement. He believed that this could not be achieved without bringing Hindus and Muslims closer. He therefore saw the Khilafat issue as an opportunity to bring Muslims into a unified national movement.

Statement 4 — Incorrect:
The Non-Cooperation programme was planned in stages. It initially included the surrender of government-awarded titles and the boycott of civil services, army, police, courts, legislative councils, schools and foreign goods.

Full civil disobedience was proposed as a later stage if the government responded with repression.
🎯 EXAM POINT

Jallianwala Bagh → Withdrawal of Rowlatt Satyagraha → Khilafat Issue → Hindu-Muslim Unity → Non-Cooperation Movement

📌 The Non-Cooperation Movement was planned in stages; full civil disobedience was not the immediate first step.

📝 DAILY ADVANCED MCQ – 02 History
With reference to the Non-Cooperation Movement in the towns, consider the following statements:

1. The movement initially received significant participation from the urban middle classes.

2. The Justice Party in Madras supported the boycott of council elections.

3. The boycott of foreign cloth contributed to a significant decline in its imports between 1921 and 1922.

4. The movement in the cities gradually slowed partly because khadi was often more expensive than mass-produced mill cloth.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A — 1, 3 and 4 only
Statement 1 — Correct:
The Non-Cooperation Movement in the towns initially attracted substantial participation from the middle classes. Students left government schools and colleges, teachers and headmasters resigned, and lawyers gave up their legal practices.

Statement 2 — Incorrect:
The Justice Party in Madras did not support the boycott of council elections. It was a non-Brahman party and believed that entering the councils could provide an opportunity for gaining political power, which was generally dominated by Brahmans.

Statement 3 — Correct:
The boycott of foreign goods had a major economic impact. The import of foreign cloth was reduced by half between 1921 and 1922, with its value falling from approximately Rs 102 crore to Rs 57 crore.

Statement 4 — Correct:
The movement gradually slowed in the cities partly because khadi was often more expensive than mass-produced mill cloth. Poor people therefore found it difficult to continue boycotting cheaper mill-made cloth.
🎯 EXAM POINT

Non-Cooperation in Towns:

Students → Schools/Colleges Boycott
Teachers → Resignation
Lawyers → Legal Practice Boycott
Merchants → Foreign Goods Boycott
People → Foreign Cloth Boycott
Liquor Shops → Picketing

📌 Key Fact: Import of foreign cloth: Rs 102 crore (1921) ↓ Rs 57 crore (1922)

📌 Why did the urban movement slow down?
Khadi was often expensive and the boycott of British institutions also created practical difficulties because suitable alternatives were not immediately available.
