Jallianwala Bagh & Non-Cooperation Movement
📝 DAILY ADVANCED MCQ – 01 History
With reference to the events following the Jallianwala Bagh incident and the beginning of the Non-Cooperation-Khilafat Movement, consider the following statements:

1. General Dyer ordered firing at Jallianwala Bagh with the stated intention of creating terror and awe among the satyagrahis.

2. Mahatma Gandhi called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha after violence spread in different parts of the country.

3. Gandhi considered the Khilafat issue as an opportunity to bring Hindus and Muslims closer and build a broader-based national movement.

4. The Non-Cooperation programme adopted at Nagpur in December 1920 immediately began with a full-scale civil disobedience campaign.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A — 1, 2 and 3 only
Statement 1 — Correct:
General Dyer ordered firing on the crowd gathered at Jallianwala Bagh. He later stated that his objective was to produce a “moral effect” and create a feeling of terror and awe among the satyagrahis.

Statement 2 — Correct:
As violence spread following the Jallianwala Bagh incident and the government responded with brutal repression, Mahatma Gandhi called off the Rowlatt Satyagraha.

Statement 3 — Correct:
Gandhi wanted to launch a more broad-based national movement. He believed that this could not be achieved without bringing Hindus and Muslims closer. He therefore saw the Khilafat issue as an opportunity to bring Muslims into a unified national movement.

Statement 4 — Incorrect:
The Non-Cooperation programme was planned in stages. It initially included the surrender of government-awarded titles and the boycott of civil services, army, police, courts, legislative councils, schools and foreign goods.

Full civil disobedience was proposed as a later stage if the government responded with repression.
🎯 EXAM POINT

Jallianwala Bagh → Withdrawal of Rowlatt Satyagraha → Khilafat Issue → Hindu-Muslim Unity → Non-Cooperation Movement

📌 The Non-Cooperation Movement was planned in stages; full civil disobedience was not the immediate first step.

📝 DAILY ADVANCED MCQ – 02 History
With reference to the Non-Cooperation Movement in the towns, consider the following statements:

1. The movement initially received significant participation from the urban middle classes.

2. The Justice Party in Madras supported the boycott of council elections.

3. The boycott of foreign cloth contributed to a significant decline in its imports between 1921 and 1922.

4. The movement in the cities gradually slowed partly because khadi was often more expensive than mass-produced mill cloth.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
✅ Correct Answer: A — 1, 3 and 4 only
Statement 1 — Correct:
The Non-Cooperation Movement in the towns initially attracted substantial participation from the middle classes. Students left government schools and colleges, teachers and headmasters resigned, and lawyers gave up their legal practices.

Statement 2 — Incorrect:
The Justice Party in Madras did not support the boycott of council elections. It was a non-Brahman party and believed that entering the councils could provide an opportunity for gaining political power, which was generally dominated by Brahmans.

Statement 3 — Correct:
The boycott of foreign goods had a major economic impact. The import of foreign cloth was reduced by half between 1921 and 1922, with its value falling from approximately Rs 102 crore to Rs 57 crore.

Statement 4 — Correct:
The movement gradually slowed in the cities partly because khadi was often more expensive than mass-produced mill cloth. Poor people therefore found it difficult to continue boycotting cheaper mill-made cloth.
🎯 EXAM POINT

Non-Cooperation in Towns:

Students → Schools/Colleges Boycott
Teachers → Resignation
Lawyers → Legal Practice Boycott
Merchants → Foreign Goods Boycott
People → Foreign Cloth Boycott
Liquor Shops → Picketing

📌 Key Fact: Import of foreign cloth: Rs 102 crore (1921) ↓ Rs 57 crore (1922)

📌 Why did the urban movement slow down?
Khadi was often expensive and the boycott of British institutions also created practical difficulties because suitable alternatives were not immediately available.
