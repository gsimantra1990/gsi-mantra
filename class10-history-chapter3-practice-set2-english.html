Part E – Source / Passage-Based Questions
Passage 1:

The nineteenth century saw major changes in the movement of goods, people and capital across the world. Railways, steamships and the telegraph reduced distances and connected markets. Food could be transported over long distances, while millions of people migrated in search of work and better opportunities. These changes contributed to the emergence of a more integrated world economy.

Passage 2:

During the Great Depression, agricultural prices fell sharply. Farmers and producers found it difficult to sell their products at profitable prices. In colonial India, peasants and cash-crop producers were affected by the fall in agricultural prices and declining demand. The crisis therefore had important consequences for rural society and the colonial economy.
