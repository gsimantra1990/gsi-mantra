Before factories became common, merchants often supplied raw materials to rural households. Peasants and artisans produced goods in their homes and supplied them to merchants. This system allowed merchants to expand production without directly establishing large factories. It also provided rural families with an additional source of income.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Proto-industrialisation refers to the phase before factories became widespread, when merchants organised production through rural households and artisans.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
Urban guilds often controlled production and restricted entry. Merchants therefore organised production in the countryside where labour was more easily available.
Q3. Correct Answer: B
James Hargreaves is associated with the invention of the Spinning Jenny.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
James Watt made important improvements to the steam engine, which became an important source of power for industrial production.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
The Spinning Jenny allowed a single worker to spin several threads at the same time, greatly increasing productivity.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Hand labour remained important because many products required detailed, flexible and specialised work.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Cotton textiles became a major symbol of British industrialisation.
Q8. Correct Answer: B
Hand production was particularly useful for goods requiring variety, precision and detailed finishing.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Bombay became an important centre of cotton textile production in colonial India.
Q10. Correct Answer: B
Cheap machine-made British cloth competed strongly with Indian handloom products.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
The Spinning Jenny came in the eighteenth century, Watt's improvements followed, Bombay's first cotton mill came in the nineteenth century, and the First World War began in 1914.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Proto-industrialisation preceded factories, followed by industrial growth in India and then the First World War.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Hargreaves — Spinning Jenny; Watt — steam engine improvement; Arkwright — Water Frame; Kay — Flying Shuttle.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Gomasthas supervised weavers for Company agents, jobbers recruited workers, industrialists invested in industries, and weavers produced cloth.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Both statements are true. The need for variety, precision and flexible production explains why hand labour continued alongside factories.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
Both statements are true. British machine-made textiles created strong competition for Indian textile producers.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
The First World War increased demand for Indian industrial products because British industries were heavily occupied with war production.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Advertisements helped producers create demand and establish recognition for their products.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
Merchants used rural households because they provided labour and helped merchants overcome some restrictions imposed by urban guilds.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
British industrialisation benefited from capital, markets, raw materials, colonial connections and technological innovations.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
The East India Company wanted a regular supply of Indian textiles for export and therefore attempted to control the production process.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Advertisements created demand, promoted brands and helped producers distinguish their products from competitors.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Indian entrepreneurs invested in industries because expanding markets and industrial production created new economic opportunities.
Q24. Correct Answer: B
Before factories became widespread, production often took place in rural households.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Merchants supplied raw materials to rural producers and collected finished goods.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Household production provided rural families with an additional source of income.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
British industries were heavily engaged in war production, reducing the availability of British manufactured goods for export to India.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Indian factories received increased demand for several products needed during the war.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
The war created opportunities for some Indian industries to expand because imports from Britain declined and demand increased.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation was a gradual process. Machines and factories expanded, but hand production, small workshops and older production systems continued alongside them.
