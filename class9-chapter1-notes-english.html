Chapter 1: Understanding Social Science

Detailed English Medium Notes | New NCERT 2026–27

1. Introduction to Social Science

Social Science is the systematic study of human society and the different ways in which people live, interact, organise themselves and respond to their surroundings.

It helps us understand not only what happens in society, but also why it happens, how it changes over time and how different factors influence one another.

Key Idea: Social Science helps us understand people, places, institutions, resources, cultures, economies, governments and the relationships among them.

Human beings do not live in isolation. Our lives are connected with families, communities, institutions, markets, governments, technology and the natural environment.

2. Social Science in Everyday Life

Many things that we use every day are part of systems created, maintained or influenced by society.

  • The house in which we live.
  • The water that we use.
  • The roads on which we travel.
  • The school where we study.
  • The markets where we buy goods.
  • The electricity and communication systems we depend upon.
  • The digital spaces and technologies we use.

Social Science helps us understand how these systems developed, how they function and how they affect different groups of people.

Example: A new road may improve transportation, increase trade, create employment and change settlement patterns. Therefore, one event can have geographical, economic, political and social effects.

3. Understanding Society through Time

Human society has changed greatly over thousands of years. Early human communities depended directly on nature for survival. Gradually, humans learned new skills and developed more complex ways of living.

  • Development of agriculture
  • Domestication of animals
  • Development of tools
  • Permanent settlements
  • Exchange and trade
  • Development of systems of governance
  • Growth of villages, towns and cities
  • Development and exchange of ideas and cultures

Studying these changes helps us understand continuity as well as change in human society.

4. India's Knowledge Traditions

The chapter highlights that the spirit of inquiry has deep roots in India's knowledge traditions. Early thinkers used questioning, discussion, observation, reasoning and reflection to understand nature, society and human life.

Important Indian ideas discussed in the chapter:

1. Pañchamahābhūtas
2. Vasudhaiva Kuṭumbakam
3. Arthaśāstra and early thinking about governance

5. Pañchamahābhūtas – The Five Great Elements

The concept of Pañchamahābhūtas comes from Indian philosophical traditions. It describes the natural world as being composed of five fundamental elements.

These five elements represent an early philosophical attempt to understand nature as an interconnected system.

The idea has been used to explain natural processes, the human body and the relationship between living beings and their environment.

Connection with Social Science: The relationship between humans and the environment is important for understanding settlement patterns, occupations, food habits, architecture, health practices and responses to environmental challenges.

6. Vasudhaiva Kuṭumbakam

"The World Is One Family"

The expression Vasudhaiva Kuṭumbakam expresses the idea that the world is one family.

It highlights the interconnectedness and interdependence of human societies across different regions and cultures.

Social Science Connection: Global trade, migration, communication, climate change, technology and international relations demonstrate how societies are connected with one another.

7. Arthaśāstra and Early Thinking about Governance

The chapter refers to the Arthaśāstra, attributed to Kauṭilya, as an important example of early systematic thinking about governance and economy in India.

Composed about 2,300 years ago, the work discusses several aspects of administration and public affairs.

  • Administration
  • Economic management
  • Taxation
  • Governance
  • Responsibilities of rulers
  • Public welfare

It demonstrates that systematic thinking about governance and economic management existed long before modern academic disciplines developed.

8. How Social Scientists Study Society

Modern Social Science uses systematic methods to study society. Researchers collect evidence, analyse information and develop explanations.

Method Purpose
Observation To carefully study people, places and events.
Interviews To collect information directly from people.
Surveys To collect information from a larger group.
Documents To examine written and recorded evidence.
Comparison To compare different places, societies or periods.
Evidence To support explanations with reliable information.
Empirical Evidence: Empirical evidence is information obtained through actual observation, investigation or examination of facts.

9. Social Science as a Group of Related Disciplines

Human society is complex. No single discipline can explain every aspect of society. Therefore, Social Science is a group of related disciplines.

Discipline Main Focus
Geography Studies places, Earth, environment and relationships between people and their surroundings.
History Studies the human past, continuity, change and developments through time.
Political Science Studies government, political institutions, power, rights, responsibilities and governance.
Economics Studies production, distribution, exchange, consumption and the use of limited resources.

10. Interconnection among the Four Disciplines

The four disciplines are different, but they are closely connected. A single event may have geographical, historical, political and economic dimensions.

Example: Drought
  • Geography: Studies rainfall, water availability and environmental conditions.
  • Economics: Studies effects on crops, farmers' income, prices and employment.
  • Political Science: Studies government policies and relief measures.
  • History/Social Studies: Studies migration and how communities have responded to drought over time.

Therefore, combining different disciplines gives us a more complete understanding of society.

11. Geography

Geography studies the Earth, places, environments and the relationship between human beings and their surroundings.

Important Questions in Geography

  • Where is a place located?
  • What physical features are found there?
  • How does the environment influence human life?
  • How do humans modify their environment?

Important Tools

  • Maps
  • Globes
  • Atlases
  • GIS – Geographic Information System
  • Infographics and spatial data

12. History

History studies the human past and helps us understand how societies have changed and developed over time.

Sources of History

  • Archaeological remains
  • Monuments and buildings
  • Coins
  • Inscriptions
  • Paintings and sculptures
  • Written documents
  • Travel accounts
  • Memoirs and correspondence
  • Oral traditions
  • Folklore
Empirical approach in History: Historians use evidence from different sources to reconstruct and interpret the past.

13. Political Science

Political Science studies government, political institutions, power, rights, duties, responsibilities and governance.

Important Areas

  • Government
  • Democracy
  • Citizenship
  • Rights and responsibilities
  • Political institutions
  • Public policy
  • Governance

In a democracy, citizens play an active role in public life. Understanding political systems helps citizens participate responsibly in society.

14. Economics

Economics studies how individuals and societies use limited resources to satisfy their needs and wants.

Major Areas

  • Production
  • Distribution
  • Exchange
  • Consumption
  • Resources
  • Employment
  • Markets
  • Economic choices
Basic Economic Problem: Resources are limited, while human wants are many. Therefore, individuals and societies have to make choices.

15. Why Should We Study Social Science?

  • To understand society.
  • To understand our past.
  • To understand our environment.
  • To understand government and citizenship.
  • To understand economic activities.
  • To appreciate diversity and inclusion.
  • To develop critical and analytical thinking.
  • To make informed decisions.
  • To understand social change.
  • To become responsible citizens.

18. Important Questions for Examination

Q1. What is Social Science?

Social Science is the systematic study of human society and the relationships among people, institutions, environment, economy and culture.

Q2. What are the four core disciplines of Social Science?

Geography, History, Political Science and Economics.

Q3. What are Pañchamahābhūtas?

Pañchamahābhūtas are the five great elements described in Indian philosophical traditions: Earth, Water, Fire, Air and Space.

Q4. What is the meaning of Vasudhaiva Kuṭumbakam?

It means “The world is one family” and expresses the interconnectedness of human societies.

Q5. What is the importance of the Arthaśāstra?

The Arthaśāstra, attributed to Kauṭilya, provides an early example of systematic thinking about administration, taxation, economic management and the responsibilities of rulers.

Q6. Why is Social Science called a group of related disciplines?

Human society is complex. Geography, History, Political Science and Economics study different dimensions of society but are closely interconnected. Together they provide a more complete understanding of human life.
