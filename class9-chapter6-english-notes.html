Chapter 6 – Democracy

Chapter Introduction:
Democracy is a system of government in which people play an important role in choosing and controlling their government. In a modern democracy, citizens generally elect representatives who form the government and remain accountable to the people.

1. Meaning of Democracy

The word democracy means government by the people. In a democracy, the ultimate source of political authority is the people.

In modern democratic countries, citizens usually elect their representatives through elections. These representatives form the government and are accountable to the people.

2. Main Features of Democracy

  • Participation of citizens.
  • Free and fair elections.
  • Rule of law.
  • Equal political rights.
  • Respect for majority opinion.
  • Protection of minority rights.
  • Accountability of government.
  • Protection of citizens' rights and freedoms.

3. Types of Democracy

Democracy can broadly be understood as direct democracy and representative democracy.

Direct Democracy: In this system, citizens directly participate in decision-making.

Representative Democracy: In this system, citizens elect representatives who make decisions and govern on their behalf.

4. Roots of Democracy in India

India has a long history of collective decision-making and political institutions. Ancient Indian traditions included various assemblies, councils and republican or gana-sangha traditions.

The Vajji or Vrijji confederacy, with Vaishali as an important centre, is often discussed as an example of an early republican tradition in ancient India.

These traditions demonstrate that ideas of discussion, consultation and collective decision-making have deep roots in Indian history.

5. Modern Indian Democracy

After independence, India adopted a democratic system of government. The Constitution established the principles of justice, liberty, equality and democratic participation.

India follows universal adult franchise, allowing adult citizens to participate in elections and choose their representatives.

6. Challenges to Democracy in India

Democracy is not limited to conducting elections. A successful democracy requires active citizen participation, strong institutions, respect for law and protection of rights.

  • Social and economic inequalities.
  • Political corruption.
  • Politics based on caste and religion.
  • Influence of money and muscle power.
  • Unequal political participation.
  • Misinformation and rumours.

7. Democratic Systems in the World

Different democratic countries have different constitutional arrangements. Two important forms of democratic government are the parliamentary system and the presidential system.

8. Parliamentary System

In a parliamentary system, the government is accountable to the legislature. The Prime Minister is generally the head of government and usually leads the party or coalition that commands a majority in the legislature.

India and Canada are examples of parliamentary democracies.

9. Presidential System

In a presidential system, the President is the head of the executive and generally exercises executive authority according to the constitution. The President's tenure is normally fixed.

The United States of America is a major example of a presidential system.

10. Parliamentary System vs Presidential System

  • In a parliamentary system, the Prime Minister is the head of government, while in a presidential system the President is the head of the executive.
  • In a parliamentary system, the executive is accountable to the legislature.
  • In a presidential system, the President normally has a fixed term of office.
  • India follows a parliamentary system, while the United States follows a presidential system.

Quick Revision

Remember:
Democracy = People's Participation + Elections + Rights + Accountability + Rule of Law

Important Terms

  • Democracy: A system of government in which people exercise political power.
  • Direct Democracy: A system in which citizens participate directly in decision-making.
  • Representative Democracy: A system in which elected representatives govern on behalf of citizens.
  • Parliamentary System: A system in which the executive is accountable to the legislature.
  • Presidential System: A system in which the President is the head of the executive.
  • Accountability: The responsibility of a government or institution to explain and justify its actions.
