Part F — Passage-Based Questions
Read the passage carefully:

Before factories became common, many merchants organised production through rural households. They supplied raw materials to peasants and artisans and collected the finished goods. This system allowed merchants to increase production without establishing large factories. Rural families could combine agricultural work with manufacturing, while merchants could supply goods to expanding markets.

Q26. Why did merchants organise production in the countryside?

Q27. What advantage did rural households have in this system?

Read the passage carefully:

The expansion of factories changed the lives of workers. Factory owners required workers to follow fixed working hours and strict discipline. Many workers worked for long hours and received low wages. Employment was also uncertain because demand for goods could change. At the same time, industrialisation created new employment opportunities and contributed to the growth of towns and cities.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Industrialisation refers to the growth of machine-based and factory-based production along with major changes in the organisation of work and production.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
Britain was the first country where industrialisation developed on a large scale during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
Cotton textiles became one of the leading sectors of Britain's early industrialisation.
Q4. Correct Answer: B
Urban craft and trade guilds often restricted entry of new producers. Merchants therefore moved production into the countryside.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
Proto-industrialisation refers to large-scale production for markets before the widespread establishment of factories.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
The Spinning Jenny enabled one worker to spin several threads at the same time, greatly increasing productivity.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
James Hargreaves is credited with inventing the Spinning Jenny.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Richard Arkwright developed the Water Frame, which used water power for spinning.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Growing demand for cotton textiles in domestic and overseas markets encouraged the expansion of the industry.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Factories made it possible to bring machines and workers together and organise production on a larger scale.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
Workers feared that machines could reduce the demand for skilled labour and threaten their employment.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Luddites were workers who protested against the introduction of certain machines, particularly when they believed machines threatened their livelihoods.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
There was often a large supply of workers seeking employment, which weakened workers' bargaining power and contributed to low wages.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Jobbers were people who recruited workers for industrialists and often helped employers obtain labour.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Early factories commonly imposed long working hours and strict discipline on workers.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
Bombay became a major centre of cotton textile production in India.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
Indian textile producers faced restrictions and increasing competition from machine-made British textiles.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Indian weavers were skilled artisans who produced textiles using traditional handloom techniques.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
The expansion of British machine-made cloth and changes in international markets created serious challenges for Indian handloom producers.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Some handloom weavers survived by producing specialised textiles and responding to changing consumer demand.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation did not immediately eliminate hand production. Hand production remained important where specialised skills, small quantities or flexibility were required.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Mechanised British factories could produce large quantities of cloth, creating strong competition for Indian textile producers.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Factory production depended on regular schedules and disciplined work, so factory owners enforced fixed working hours.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
The developments listed broadly follow the early sequence of the Spinning Jenny, Water Frame, improvements to the steam engine and later developments in power weaving.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
James Hargreaves — Spinning Jenny; Richard Arkwright — Water Frame; James Watt — improvements to the steam engine; Jobber — recruitment of workers.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
Merchants used rural production networks to bypass urban guild restrictions and increase the quantity of goods produced for markets.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Rural families could combine agricultural activities with manufacturing work, allowing them to earn additional income.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Factory production introduced fixed working schedules and stronger discipline compared with many earlier forms of household production.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Many early industrial workers experienced long hours, low wages and uncertainty of employment.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Industrialisation transformed production methods, markets, urban growth and the lives and working conditions of millions of people.
