📖 Chapter Overview

The Making of a Global World explains the historical process through which different parts of the world became increasingly connected through trade, migration, technology and the movement of capital.

The chapter covers the pre-modern world, the nineteenth-century global economy, migration, indentured labour, technological developments, the Great Depression, the two World Wars and the post-war international economic system.

📚 Important Topics

1. The Pre-modern World

Early trade, travel, migration and cultural connections between different parts of the world.

2. Silk Routes Link the World

Historical trade routes that connected Asia, Europe and other regions through trade and cultural exchange.

3. Food Travels

The movement of food crops across continents and their impact on societies.

4. Conquest, Disease and Trade

European expansion and its impact on societies and economies.

5. Nineteenth-Century Global Economy

Trade, migration, labour and capital flows during the nineteenth century.

6. Corn Laws

The Corn Laws and their abolition helped change Britain's food trade and the global economy.

7. Indentured Labour

The migration of workers under fixed-term contracts to different colonies.

8. Technology and Globalisation

Railways, steamships, canals and communication technologies transformed global connections.

9. The Great Depression

The economic crisis of the 1930s and its impact on trade, agriculture and employment.

10. Post-war Reconstruction

Bretton Woods institutions and the emergence of a new international economic system.
