The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

Detailed English Notes
Central Theme:
This chapter explains how the idea of nationalism developed in Europe and how it transformed the political map of Europe through revolutions, national movements and the formation of nation-states.
Nation-State Sorrieu French Revolution Napoleon Liberalism Conservatism Germany Italy Britain Culture Balkans Revision Questions

1. What is a Nation-State?

A nation-state is a modern political system in which the political boundaries of a state are closely associated with the identity of a nation. People develop a sense of belonging to a common national community.

This common identity may develop through shared history, culture, language, traditions, political ideas and collective memories.

In simple words:
A nation-state is a state in which people develop a strong sense of belonging to one nation and identify themselves as members of that national community.

Major Features of a Nation-State

  • A common national identity.
  • A defined political territory.
  • A sense of common citizenship.
  • Shared historical and cultural memories.
  • A political relationship between citizens and the state.
Important: The nation-state did not emerge suddenly. It developed through a long historical process in Europe.

2. Frederic Sorrieu and His 1848 Series

In 1848, the French artist Frederic Sorrieu prepared a series of four prints visualising his dream of a world made up of democratic and social republics.

The prints reflected the revolutionary and nationalist ideas that were spreading across Europe during the nineteenth century.

Understanding the Four Prints

Print 1

Vision of Free Nations

The prints present a vision of peoples and nations moving towards a future based on freedom, democracy and national identity.

Print 2

Procession of Nations

People representing different nations appear with their national flags. The procession symbolises national identity and equality among nations.

Print 3

Journey towards Liberty

The imagined movement away from old oppressive political systems represents the desire for freedom and democratic government.

Print 4

A Democratic Future

The final vision represents a future in which nations are free and people live according to democratic principles.

Main Message: Sorrieu imagined a future of free nations, democratic government and national equality.

Important Symbols

  • Statue of Liberty: Represents freedom and democratic ideals.
  • National Flags: Represent national identity.
  • Procession of People: Represents the collective movement towards freedom and nationalism.
  • Signs of Old Political Order: Represent the decline of oppressive systems.

3. The French Revolution and Nationalism

The French Revolution of 1789 played a major role in the development of modern nationalism. It challenged monarchy and linked political authority with the idea of citizens and the nation.

France before the Revolution

  • France was ruled by a monarchy.
  • Society was divided into estates and privileges.
  • The king possessed considerable political power.
  • The Third Estate carried a heavy tax burden.

Major Stages and Events

1789 — Beginning of the Revolution

The meeting of the Estates-General and the formation of the National Assembly by the Third Estate gave a new direction to the revolution. The Bastille was stormed on 14 July 1789.

1789 — Declaration of Rights

The Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen emphasised liberty, equality and rights.

1791 — Constitution

France adopted a constitutional monarchy. The powers of the king were limited and the legislature gained greater importance.

1792 — Republic

The monarchy was abolished and France became a republic.

1793–94 — Jacobin Phase

The revolution entered a radical phase under the leadership of Maximilian Robespierre. This period is associated with the Reign of Terror.

1794 — Fall of Robespierre

Robespierre was removed from power and the Jacobin phase came to an end.

1795–99 — Directory

The Directory governed France during a period of political instability.

1799 — Rise of Napoleon

Napoleon Bonaparte seized political power and began a new phase in French history.

How Did the Revolution Promote Nationalism?

  • The idea of citizenship was strengthened.
  • National symbols were promoted.
  • Political authority was connected with the nation.
  • The idea of equality before law gained importance.
  • A common national identity was encouraged.

4. Napoleon Bonaparte and His Impact

Napoleon Bonaparte was a famous French military leader and ruler. He extended French influence over large parts of Europe.

Major Reforms Introduced by Napoleon

  • Law codes were organised and standardised.
  • Birth-based privileges were weakened.
  • Equality before law was promoted.
  • Administrative systems were reorganised.
  • Feudal restrictions were reduced in many areas.

The Napoleonic Code

The Civil Code of 1804: The Napoleonic Code gave importance to equality before law, protection of property rights and a more uniform legal system.

Positive Effects of Napoleon

  • Greater uniformity in law.
  • Reduction of feudal privileges.
  • Protection of property rights.
  • Modern administrative reforms.

Drawbacks and Limitations

Napoleon was not only a reformer. His rule was also connected with military expansion and imperial domination. In many conquered regions, people experienced foreign rule, taxation and economic pressure.

  • Continuous military expansion.
  • French control over several European territories.
  • Economic and tax burdens.
  • Resistance against foreign domination.
Conclusion: Napoleon spread important legal and administrative reforms, but his imperial expansion also encouraged nationalist resistance against French domination.

5. Liberalism

Liberalism was an important political idea associated with nationalist movements in nineteenth-century Europe. It emphasised individual freedom, equality before law and representative government.

Liberalism in the Political Sphere

  • Individual freedom.
  • Equality before law.
  • Representative government.
  • Constitutional rule.
  • Opposition to arbitrary and autocratic power.

Liberalism in the Economic Sphere

  • Reduction of unnecessary restrictions on trade.
  • Freedom of economic activity.
  • Expansion of markets and trade.
  • Removal of feudal economic barriers.
Important: Political liberalism at that time did not mean universal adult franchise. Women and property-less men were generally excluded from political rights in many European societies.

6. Conservatism and Revolutionary Movements

After 1815, powerful European monarchies attempted to preserve the old political order. This approach is associated with conservatism.

However, liberal and nationalist ideas continued to spread. Revolutionary movements in different parts of Europe, especially around 1830 and 1848, challenged old political systems.

Conservatives

Supported traditional institutions, monarchy and the existing social and political order.

Liberals

Supported individual freedom, constitutional government and political representation.

7. The Unification of Germany

At the beginning of the nineteenth century, German-speaking regions were divided into many states. Nationalist ideas gradually strengthened the demand for political unity.

Role of Prussia

Prussia played the leading role in German unification. Its military and political strength was used to bring different German states together.

Otto von Bismarck

Bismarck was the chief architect of German unification. He used diplomacy, military power and political strategy to achieve the goal.

1864

War against Denmark.

1866

War against Austria.

1870–71

War against France.

1871

The German Empire was proclaimed.

Conclusion: German unification was completed with the establishment of the German Empire in 1871.

8. The Unification of Italy

In the nineteenth century, Italy was divided into several states and territories. Nationalist movements gradually strengthened the idea of a united Italy.

Giuseppe Mazzini

Mazzini was an important nationalist thinker and revolutionary. He worked for a united and independent Italy.

Count Camillo de Cavour

Cavour was the chief minister of Sardinia-Piedmont. He used diplomacy, alliances and political strategy to advance Italian unification.

Giuseppe Garibaldi

Garibaldi was a famous nationalist revolutionary. His military campaigns helped bring southern Italy into the process of unification.

1859

Important success in the struggle against Austria.

1860

Garibaldi led his campaign in southern Italy.

1861

The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed.

1866

Venetia became part of Italy.

1870

Rome became part of Italy and the unification process was completed.

9. The Making of the Nation-State in Britain

The formation of the British nation-state was different from the unification of Germany and Italy. It was the result of a long political, parliamentary and constitutional process.

The Dominant Role of England

England played the dominant role in this process. Wales, Scotland and Ireland were incorporated through different political developments.

Act of Union, 1707

The Act of Union of 1707 united England and Scotland politically and created the Kingdom of Great Britain.

Ireland

Ireland was incorporated into the United Kingdom in 1801. However, Irish opposition and national aspirations continued.

Important Difference: Germany and Italy were unified largely through nationalist movements, political struggles and wars, while Britain developed through a longer process of political and parliamentary union.

10. Nationalism and Culture

Nationalism did not develop only through political movements. Art, literature, music, folk traditions and language also played an important role.

Role of Language

Local languages were often connected with national identity. Language helped people see themselves as members of a common cultural community.

Folk Culture

Folk songs, folk tales and local traditions helped preserve memories of a common past and strengthened national feeling.

Romanticism

Romantic thinkers and artists emphasised emotions, folk culture and the shared historical past. These ideas helped strengthen nationalist feelings.

11. The Balkan Region

The Balkans was a region of south-eastern Europe inhabited by many different ethnic, linguistic and religious communities. This diversity made its politics particularly complex.

The Ottoman Empire

Large parts of the Balkans remained under the control of the Ottoman Empire for a long period. During the nineteenth century, the Ottoman Empire gradually weakened.

Growth of Nationalism in the Balkans

  • Growth of national identities among different communities.
  • Demand for independent nation-states.
  • Weakening of the Ottoman Empire.
  • Competition among European powers.
  • Conflicts over territory and political control.

Why Did Tensions Increase?

Different communities wanted to create independent states. This created conflicts over territory and political control. At the same time, major European powers such as Russia, Germany and Austria-Hungary had their own interests in the region.

Main Point: Nationalism, imperial rivalry and territorial conflicts made the Balkans one of the most unstable regions of nineteenth-century Europe.

12. National Allegories and Symbols

European nations were often represented through female allegorical figures. These figures were not real persons; they represented the abstract idea of the nation.

Marianne — France

Marianne represented the French nation and republican values. She became associated with liberty, the Republic and national identity.

Germania — Germany

Germania represented the German nation. Her symbols expressed national unity, strength, freedom and defence of the nation.

Important Symbols of Germania

Symbol Meaning
Crown of oak leaves Heroism and strength.
Broken chains Freedom from bondage.
Sword Readiness to defend the nation.
Black-red-gold flag German nationalist identity and unity.
Shield National protection and defence.

Important Symbols of Marianne

  • Red cap — associated with liberty and revolutionary traditions.
  • Tricolour — symbol of French national identity.
  • Liberty and republican ideals.

16. Final Conclusion

The development of modern nationalism in Europe was a long historical process. The French Revolution gave new political meaning to the ideas of citizenship and the nation. Napoleon's reforms influenced modern law and administration, while his imperial expansion also created resistance.

During the nineteenth century, liberalism, nationalism and revolutionary movements challenged the old political order. The unification of Germany and Italy transformed nationalist ideas into political states, while Britain followed a different path of political and parliamentary union.

In the Balkans, nationalism, imperial rivalry and territorial conflicts created increasing political tension. Thus, nationalism played a major role in reshaping the political map of Europe and in creating the modern nation-state.
