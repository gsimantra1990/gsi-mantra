📜 The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

Class 10 History • Chapter 1

GSI MANTRA • English Notes

📖 Chapter Introduction

The nineteenth century witnessed the emergence of nationalism as a powerful political force in Europe. Before the rise of modern nation-states, Europe was divided into kingdoms, empires, principalities and territories ruled by different dynasties.

The French Revolution, Napoleonic reforms, liberalism, conservatism, revolutionary movements and the unification of Italy and Germany transformed the political map of Europe.

1. What is a Nation-State?

A nation-state is a state in which people develop a common national identity and regard themselves as members of a particular nation. This identity may be based on shared history, culture, language, territory or political aspirations.

Remember: Nationalism is the feeling of belonging to a common nation and the desire for national unity, political freedom and self-government.

2. The French Revolution and Nationalism

The French Revolution of 1789 was one of the most important events in the development of modern nationalism.

Major Ideas

  • Liberty
  • Equality
  • Fraternity
  • Popular sovereignty
  • Citizenship and equal rights

The revolution transferred the idea of sovereignty from the monarch to the people. France attempted to create a common national identity through uniform laws, administration and national symbols.

Impact on Europe

  • National identity became politically important.
  • Ideas of citizenship and equal rights spread.
  • Feudal privileges were challenged.
  • Ideas of liberty and nationalism spread to other European regions.

3. Frédéric Sorrieu's Vision

In 1848, the French artist Frédéric Sorrieu prepared a series of four prints visualising a world made up of democratic and social republics.

Important Features

  • People from different nations are shown marching with flags and national costumes.
  • The Statue of Liberty appears as a symbol of freedom.
  • The old symbols of absolutist institutions are shown being pushed aside.
  • The peoples of Europe are imagined moving towards a democratic future.
Important: Sorrieu's prints were an artistic and imagined vision of a democratic world, not an actual political map of Europe.

4. Rise of Napoleon Bonaparte

Napoleon Bonaparte emerged as a powerful military leader after the French Revolution. His military victories helped him gain enormous political influence.

In 1799, Napoleon seized political power and became First Consul. In 1804, he declared himself Emperor of France.

Major Reforms

  • Administrative reforms
  • Legal reforms
  • Abolition of many feudal privileges
  • More uniform administration
  • Reorganisation of territories under French control

5. The Napoleonic Code

The Civil Code of 1804, commonly known as the Napoleonic Code, established important principles of legal equality and protection of private property.

Main Features

  • Equality before the law.
  • Abolition of privileges based on birth.
  • Protection of private property.
  • More rational and uniform administration.

However, Napoleon's rule was not democratic in the modern sense. His military expansion and domination of European territories also generated resistance.

6. Greek War of Independence

Greece had been part of the Ottoman Empire. During the nineteenth century, Greek nationalism grew and the Greeks began a struggle for independence.

The Greek War of Independence began in 1821. European intellectuals, artists and many political groups supported the Greek cause.

Greek independence became an important example of nationalism and strengthened the idea that people could fight for an independent nation.

7. The Habsburg Empire

The Habsburg Empire was a large multi-national empire in Central Europe. It contained people speaking different languages and belonging to different cultural and ethnic communities.

Germans, Hungarians, Italians, Poles, Croats and several other groups lived within the empire. This diversity made the creation of a single national identity difficult.

8. Liberalism

Liberalism stood for individual freedom, equality before the law and representative government. It challenged absolute monarchy and inherited privileges.

Political Liberalism

  • Individual freedom
  • Equality before law
  • Representative government
  • Constitutional government
  • Civil rights

Economic Liberalism

  • Freedom of markets
  • Reduction of internal trade barriers
  • Free movement of goods and capital
  • Removal of unnecessary customs restrictions

9. Zollverein

In 1834, a customs union called the Zollverein was formed at the initiative of Prussia among many German states.

It removed many internal tariff barriers and encouraged economic cooperation and trade among German states.

Importance: Economic integration created favourable conditions for the later political unification of Germany.

10. Conservative Forces

Conservatives generally supported traditional institutions such as monarchy, social hierarchy and established property relations. They feared sudden revolutionary changes.

After 1815, conservative forces attempted to restore old dynasties and suppress revolutionary and nationalist movements.

11. Giuseppe Mazzini

Giuseppe Mazzini was one of the most important Italian nationalist revolutionaries. He believed that Italy should become a united and independent republic.

He founded Young Italy and encouraged young people to work for national unity and independence.

12. Unification of Italy

Before unification, Italy was divided into several states. Some northern territories were under Austrian influence or control.

Major Leaders

  • Giuseppe Mazzini: nationalist ideology and revolutionary organisation.
  • Count Cavour: Prime Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont and an important diplomat.
  • Giuseppe Garibaldi: military leader who helped unite southern Italy.
  • Victor Emmanuel II: King of unified Italy.

Cavour used diplomacy and war against Austria. Garibaldi and his volunteers conquered territories in southern Italy and transferred them to Victor Emmanuel II.

1861: Kingdom of Italy proclaimed.

1866: Venetia became part of Italy.

1870: Rome was incorporated into Italy.

13. Unification of Germany

Germany was divided into many states. Prussia emerged as the leading power in the process of unification.

Prussian Chief Minister Otto von Bismarck used the army and diplomacy to achieve German unity.

Wars against Denmark, Austria and France helped Prussia bring the German states together.

1871: William I of Prussia was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles.

14. Formation of Britain as a Nation-State

The formation of the British nation-state was different from the revolutionary unifications of Italy and Germany. It developed gradually through political union and the expansion of English power.

The Act of Union of 1707 united England and Scotland. Ireland was incorporated into the United Kingdom in 1801.

The English language, institutions and political power became dominant within the union.

15. The Balkans

The Balkans was a region of great ethnic, linguistic and religious diversity. A large part of the region had been under Ottoman rule.

As the Ottoman Empire weakened, different national groups demanded independence and created their own national movements.

Reasons for Conflict

  • Growth of nationalist movements.
  • Decline of the Ottoman Empire.
  • Competition among European powers.
  • Territorial ambitions.
  • Ethnic and religious diversity.

16. Imperialism and Balkan Conflicts

By the late nineteenth century, European powers competed for political influence and territory. The Balkans had strategic importance and became an area of rivalry.

Russia, Austria-Hungary, Germany and other powers had competing interests in the region. Nationalism therefore became increasingly linked with rivalry, territorial ambitions and imperialism.

17. Allegory and National Symbols

An allegory represents an abstract idea through a person or symbol. During the nineteenth century, nations were often represented through female figures.

Nation Allegorical Figure Important Symbols
France Marianne Red cap, tricolour, symbols of liberty and republic
Germany Germania Sword, oak leaves, black-red-gold colours

Marianne became a symbol of the French Republic and liberty. Germania represented German unity, strength and national identity.

18. Important Dates

1789 — French Revolution

1804 — Napoleonic Code

1815 — Congress of Vienna

1821 — Greek War of Independence begins

1834 — Zollverein formed

1848 — Revolutions in Europe and Sorrieu's prints

1861 — Kingdom of Italy proclaimed

1870 — Rome incorporated into Italy

1871 — German Empire proclaimed

19. Quick Revision

  • The French Revolution gave nationalism a new political meaning.
  • Napoleon introduced important legal and administrative reforms.
  • Liberalism promoted freedom, equality and representative government.
  • Zollverein encouraged German economic integration.
  • Mazzini promoted Italian national unity.
  • Cavour used diplomacy and war to support Italian unification.
  • Garibaldi played a major military role in southern Italy.
  • Bismarck used wars and diplomacy to unite Germany.
  • Britain developed as a nation-state through gradual political union.
  • The Balkans became a centre of nationalist and imperial rivalry.
