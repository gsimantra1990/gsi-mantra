Part E — Passage-Based Advanced Questions
Read the passage carefully:

The coming of print changed the circulation of knowledge. Earlier, manuscripts were expensive and were often produced by skilled scribes. Printing allowed texts to be reproduced in large numbers and made many forms of literature more accessible. However, access to print did not automatically mean that everyone became literate. Different groups encountered printed material through different forms, including public readings, cheap books, newspapers and religious publications.

In colonial India, newspapers, journals, pamphlets and books became important means of discussing social reform and political questions. At the same time, colonial authorities watched the press carefully and introduced measures to control publications considered politically threatening. Thus, print became both a means of public discussion and an area of political conflict.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: A
Printing allowed texts to be reproduced in much larger numbers and at lower cost than many manuscripts, contributing to the growth of a reading public.
Q2. Correct Answer: A
Print made it easier for alternative interpretations and criticisms of established religious authority to circulate among a wider audience.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
Gutenberg's innovation involved movable metal type and a printing system that made the reproduction of texts much faster and more efficient.
Q4. Correct Answer: A
Printing enabled Luther's writings to be reproduced and distributed rapidly, helping his ideas reach readers across different regions.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
The print revolution refers broadly to changes in production, circulation and consumption of texts rather than simply the invention of a machine.
Q6. Correct Answer: A
Technological improvements combined with increasing literacy created favourable conditions for the growth of newspapers and journals.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Authorities could view printed criticism and political arguments as capable of influencing public opinion, which encouraged attempts at regulation.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
The growth of vernacular printing expanded access to ideas among readers who were more familiar with Indian languages than English.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Reformers used newspapers, pamphlets and books to debate social practices, religious questions and reform ideas before wider audiences.
Q10. Correct Answer: A
Print did not simply destroy oral culture. Printed material could itself be read aloud and could interact with existing oral practices.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
The printing press developed in Europe before the Reformation's print expansion, while the major expansion of Indian print culture occurred later.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
The broad historical process moved from manuscript traditions to mechanical printing, followed by wider circulation and expanding reading publics.
Q13. Correct Answer: A
Gutenberg's printing press in Europe belongs to the fifteenth century, earlier than the nineteenth-century developments listed in the other options.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Multiple copies made it possible for a writer's ideas to travel beyond the immediate geographical location of the writer.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Print provided a platform through which reformers could present arguments and encourage public discussion about social and religious practices.
Q16. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false. Print did not always create agreement; different printed texts could encourage disagreement and debate. The reason is true.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
Colonial authorities sometimes restricted publications because printed criticism and political arguments could influence public opinion.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Gutenberg is associated with European printing technology, Luther with Reformation writings, vernacular press with local languages, and censorship with control of publications.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
Print expanded the circulation of ideas, helped create reading publics and provided a medium for discussion and debate.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Vernacular publications made printed ideas more accessible to readers who did not use elite or colonial languages.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers and publications could connect readers around common political questions and help create broader communities of discussion.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Censorship refers to attempts by authorities or institutions to restrict, control or suppress the circulation of particular information or opinions.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
The passage makes an important distinction: print expanded access to texts, but this did not mean that literacy instantly became universal.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
People who could not read themselves could encounter printed ideas when texts were read aloud in public or shared settings.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Print could reach non-literate audiences through oral reading and other shared practices, so the spread of print and literacy were related but not identical.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
The passage presents print as both a platform for public debate and a subject of colonial regulation.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Indian-language publications helped extend social and political discussions to readers who were not primarily English readers.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Attempts to monitor publications indicate that authorities considered printed communication capable of affecting political discussion and public opinion.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
A newspaper spreading criticism and generating wider discussion demonstrates how print could influence public debate.
Q30. Correct Answer: A
Print had wide historical consequences because it transformed the circulation of knowledge, religious arguments, social reform ideas and political discussions.
