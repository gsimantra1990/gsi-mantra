Section E – Passage-Based Questions

Read the passage carefully:

The nineteenth century was a period of rapid transformation in the global economy. New forms of transport, including railways and steamships, made it possible to move goods, people and raw materials over long distances more quickly. The expansion of trade created new opportunities but also produced new forms of inequality. Large numbers of people migrated in search of work and better livelihoods. India became closely connected with the world economy through the export of raw materials and agricultural products and through the movement of indentured labour.

Advanced Passage:

During the Great Depression of the 1930s, agricultural prices fell sharply in many parts of the world. India, which exported agricultural products, was deeply affected. Peasants and farmers saw the value of their crops decline, but their obligations such as revenue payments and debts did not disappear. Those who produced crops for export markets were particularly vulnerable to fluctuations in international demand and prices. The crisis therefore demonstrated how closely the Indian countryside had become linked with the wider world economy.
