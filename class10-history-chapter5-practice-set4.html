Part D — Passage Based Questions
Read the passage carefully:

The print revolution did not simply increase the number of books. It changed the manner in which people encountered ideas. Printed material could be produced in multiple copies, transported to distant places and read by people who were not directly connected with the author. This encouraged discussion, disagreement and the formation of new intellectual communities. However, access to print was still influenced by literacy, income, language and social position.

Read the passage carefully:

In colonial India, newspapers and journals became important spaces for public discussion. They reported political developments, commented on government policies and provided opportunities for people to debate social questions. At the same time, colonial authorities viewed some publications as politically dangerous and introduced measures to control the press. Thus, print became both a medium of public communication and a field of political contestation.

Test Result

Answer Key & Detailed Explanations
Q1. Correct Answer: B
Print allowed ideas to be reproduced and circulated rapidly, reaching readers beyond the immediate social or geographical circle of an author.
Q2. Correct Answer: B
Gutenberg's press made reproduction of texts considerably faster and more systematic than manual copying.
Q3. Correct Answer: A
Printed pamphlets and books helped religious arguments circulate widely during the Reformation.
Q4. Correct Answer: B
The printing of Luther's writings enabled his criticism and religious ideas to reach a much wider audience.
Q5. Correct Answer: A
Authorities could fear print because readers might encounter arguments that questioned established religious institutions and interpretations.
Q6. Correct Answer: B
Print technology increased the speed and scale of communication, thereby affecting intellectual and social life.
Q7. Correct Answer: A
Literacy was not universal, and oral traditions remained important even after printed material became widespread.
Q8. Correct Answer: A
Growing literacy together with cheaper printed material helped create a larger reading public.
Q9. Correct Answer: A
Vernacular printing made ideas accessible to people who did not read elite languages such as Latin.
Q10. Correct Answer: B
Print expanded communication but did not create agreement. It could encourage debate, disagreement and competing interpretations.
Q11. Correct Answer: A
The ability to reproduce and circulate texts widely directly contributed to the intellectual impact of print.
Q12. Correct Answer: A
Printed religious arguments could reach readers beyond a small scholarly community, increasing the scale of debate.
Q13. Correct Answer: D
The assertion is false because print did not automatically create social equality. The reason is true: literacy and access remained unequal.
Q14. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers, journals and pamphlets created platforms through which reformers could discuss social issues publicly.
Q15. Correct Answer: A
Print developed from existing manuscript traditions, followed by mechanical reproduction, wider circulation and the expansion of reading publics.
Q16. Correct Answer: A
Reproducibility made it possible for religious arguments to be produced in numerous copies and circulated widely.
Q17. Correct Answer: A
Printed books could generally be reproduced much faster and in greater numbers than individually handwritten manuscripts.
Q18. Correct Answer: A
Authorities could view widely circulated printed ideas as capable of influencing public opinion and challenging established power.
Q19. Correct Answer: A
The significance of print went beyond quantity: it changed how ideas were circulated, discussed and received.
Q20. Correct Answer: A
Literacy, income and social position affected who could access and effectively use printed material.
Q21. Correct Answer: A
Print widened communication while access to literacy and printed material remained unequal.
Q22. Correct Answer: A
Newspapers provided spaces for discussion of political developments and social issues in colonial India.
Q23. Correct Answer: A
Colonial authorities were concerned that some publications could spread political criticism and anti-colonial ideas.
Q24. Correct Answer: A
Print could become a contested space in which colonial authorities and critics competed over information and public opinion.
Q25. Correct Answer: A
Higher literacy and lower prices together make printed material accessible to a larger section of society.
Q26. Correct Answer: A
A widely circulated newspaper carrying criticism demonstrates how print can influence public political discussion.
Q27. Correct Answer: A
Print is a medium rather than an inherently reformist force. Different social and political groups could use it to circulate different viewpoints.
Q28. Correct Answer: A
Print in India became important for social reform, public discussion, political awareness and the formation of identities.
Q29. Correct Answer: A
Attempts to regulate publications demonstrate that authorities considered printed communication capable of affecting public discourse.
Q30. Correct Answer: B
The historical importance of print lies in its effects on the circulation of knowledge and its connections with religious, social and political change.
