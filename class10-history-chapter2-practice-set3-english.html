Class 10

Level Advanced
MCQ

1. Why did the First World War create economic difficulties for many Indians?

Answer: B
The war increased defence expenditure. The British government financed it through taxes and loans, while prices of essential goods increased considerably.
MCQ

2. Which statement best explains the significance of Satyagraha in Gandhi's political philosophy?

Answer: C
Satyagraha was based on truth and non-violence. Gandhi believed that a Satyagrahi could oppose injustice without using physical violence.
MCQ

3. The Champaran movement of 1917 was associated mainly with:

Answer: A
Gandhi's first major satyagraha in India was at Champaran in Bihar, where peasants were compelled to cultivate indigo under the tinkathia system.
MCQ

4. What was the major issue behind the Kheda Satyagraha?

Answer: B
In Kheda, crop failure and plague affected peasants. They demanded relaxation in revenue collection.
MCQ

5. Gandhi's Ahmedabad Mill Strike demonstrated which form of resistance?

Answer: B
Gandhi supported mill workers in Ahmedabad and undertook a fast to put moral pressure during the wage dispute.
MCQ

6. Why was the Rowlatt Act strongly opposed by Indian nationalists?

Answer: B
The Rowlatt Act gave the colonial government extensive powers to repress political activities, including detention without trial.
MCQ

7. Which event led Gandhi to withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1922?

Answer: B
The violent incident at Chauri Chaura in February 1922 led Gandhi to withdraw the movement because he believed the campaign was becoming violent.
MCQ

8. Who was an important leader of the peasant movement in Awadh?

Answer: A
Baba Ramchandra led the movement of peasants in Awadh. Peasants were unhappy with high rents, revenue demands and the practices of talukdars.
MCQ

9. What was a major demand of the peasants in Awadh?

Answer: B
Peasants demanded reduction of revenue, abolition of begar and social boycott of oppressive landlords.
MCQ

10. Why did the peasant movement in Awadh sometimes take a form different from Gandhi's idea of non-violent struggle?

Answer: B
Different social groups interpreted Swaraj according to their own local experiences and grievances. Therefore, their actions did not always exactly match the national leadership's programme.
MCQ

11. Alluri Sitarama Raju became associated with tribal resistance in:

Answer: C
Alluri Sitarama Raju led a tribal rebellion in the Gudem Hills region of present-day Andhra Pradesh.
MCQ

12. Which colonial policy particularly affected tribal communities in the Gudem Hills?

Answer: A
Forest regulations restricted shifting cultivation, grazing and access to forest resources, affecting tribal livelihoods.
MCQ

13. Which statement about Alluri Sitarama Raju is most accurate?

Answer: B
Raju became a prominent leader of the Gudem rebellion. His approach included armed resistance, which differed from Gandhi's strict commitment to non-violence.
MCQ

14. Plantation workers in Assam interpreted the idea of Swaraj mainly in relation to:

Answer: A
Plantation workers were restricted by laws such as the Inland Emigration Act. For them, Swaraj could mean freedom to move and return to their home villages.
MCQ

15. Why is the countryside important for understanding Indian nationalism?

Answer: B
The spread of nationalism into the countryside took different forms because peasants, tribal groups and plantation workers connected national ideas with their own local grievances.
MCQ

16. What was the significance of the Simon Commission's composition?

Answer: B
The Simon Commission was appointed in 1927 and had no Indian member. Its arrival in India in 1928 led to widespread protests.
MCQ

17. The Dandi March was directly connected with opposition to:

Answer: B
Gandhi chose the salt law as a symbol of colonial exploitation and began the Civil Disobedience Movement with the Dandi March in 1930.
MCQ

18. What made salt an effective symbol for the Civil Disobedience Movement?

Answer: B
Salt was a basic necessity. The tax and government monopoly on salt made it an issue that could connect people across different social and economic groups.
ASSERTION-REASON

19. Assertion (A): Different social groups interpreted the idea of Swaraj differently.

Reason (R): Their economic, social and political experiences were not identical.

Answer: A
Different communities experienced colonial rule differently. Therefore, the meaning they attached to Swaraj also varied.
ASSERTION-REASON

20. Assertion (A): Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement after Chauri Chaura.

Reason (R): Gandhi believed that mass struggle had to remain consistent with non-violence.

Answer: A
Gandhi considered non-violence fundamental to Satyagraha. The violence at Chauri Chaura convinced him that the movement needed to be withdrawn.
PASSAGE-BASED
Passage:
In the countryside, nationalist ideas did not simply arrive as a ready-made political programme. Peasants and tribal communities often connected the language of Swaraj with their immediate problems. In Awadh, peasants opposed high rents, revenue demands and the practices of talukdars. In the Gudem Hills, tribal people were affected by restrictions on their traditional use of forests. These movements became connected with the wider national struggle, but their local demands remained important.

21. What central idea is best reflected in the passage?

Answer: B
The passage shows that peasants and tribal communities connected nationalism with their own local experiences and grievances.
PASSAGE-BASED
Passage:
Gandhi's Satyagraha was based on the belief that truth and non-violence could become instruments of political struggle. A Satyagrahi was expected to resist injustice without hatred and without using physical violence. The objective was not simply to defeat an opponent but to appeal to the moral conscience of the opponent and wider society.

22. According to the passage, what distinguishes Satyagraha from violent resistance?

Answer: B
The passage describes Satyagraha as resistance to injustice through truth and non-violence.
MATCHING

23. Match the following:

A. Baba Ramchandra
B. Alluri Sitarama Raju
C. Dandi March
D. Simon Commission

1. Salt law
2. Awadh peasant movement
3. Gudem Hills tribal rebellion
4. No Indian member

Answer: A
Baba Ramchandra — Awadh; Alluri Sitarama Raju — Gudem Hills; Dandi March — Salt Law; Simon Commission — No Indian member.
CHRONOLOGICAL ARRANGEMENT

24. Arrange the following events chronologically:

1. Champaran Satyagraha
2. Rowlatt Satyagraha
3. Non-Cooperation Movement
4. Dandi March

Answer: A
Champaran — 1917; Rowlatt Satyagraha — 1919; Non-Cooperation — 1920–22; Dandi March — 1930.
CHRONOLOGICAL ARRANGEMENT

25. Arrange these events in the correct chronological order:

1. Formation of Swaraj Party
2. Arrival of Simon Commission in India
3. Dandi March
4. Chauri Chaura incident

Answer: A
Swaraj Party — 1923; Chauri Chaura — 1922. Therefore, the correct sequence actually begins with Chauri Chaura. Hence the correct chronological order is 4 → 1 → 2 → 3. Correct option: B.
MCQ

26. Which difference between Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience is most appropriate?

Answer: A
Non-Cooperation involved withdrawing cooperation from colonial institutions. Civil Disobedience went further by openly violating selected colonial laws, such as the salt law.
MCQ

27. Why did the participation of different social groups strengthen the mass character of nationalism?

Answer: B
Mass nationalism expanded as different groups connected their own struggles with the broader movement against colonial rule.
MCQ

28. Which of the following best explains why the Civil Disobedience Movement could not be understood only as a single uniform movement?

Answer: A
Different groups participated for different reasons. Their expectations from Swaraj and their economic interests were not identical.
ASSERTION-REASON

29. Assertion (A): The Dandi March became a powerful symbol of resistance.

Reason (R): The salt issue affected ordinary people and provided a simple way to challenge colonial authority.

Answer: A
Salt was a basic necessity. Breaking the salt law therefore provided an accessible symbol of opposition to colonial rule.
PASSAGE-BASED
Passage:
Indian nationalism grew through a combination of common political objectives and diverse local experiences. The idea of Swaraj could mean relief from oppressive landlords to one group, freedom from restrictive forest laws to another, and freedom from plantation restrictions to another. At the national level, leaders attempted to bring these diverse struggles into a broader movement against colonial rule. Yet these different expectations could also create tensions within the movement.

30. Which conclusion can be most logically drawn from the passage?

Answer: B
The passage highlights an important feature of Indian nationalism: different communities participated in a common anti-colonial struggle while interpreting Swaraj according to their own experiences.
