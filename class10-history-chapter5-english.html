1. The First Printed Books

The history of print begins much earlier than the invention of the modern printing press. China, Japan and Korea developed important printing traditions centuries before printing became widespread in Europe.

In China, woodblock printing was used to reproduce texts. The technology involved carving the entire page on a wooden block, applying ink to it and pressing paper against the surface.

Why was printing important?

  • It allowed texts to be reproduced in multiple copies.
  • Religious texts could reach larger audiences.
  • Knowledge could be preserved and circulated.
  • It gradually reduced dependence on handwritten manuscripts.
Board Exam Point: China was one of the earliest centres of printing technology, and woodblock printing became an important method of reproducing texts.

2. Print in China, Japan and Korea

Region Major Features
China Woodblock printing; books for scholars, officials and religious purposes; later expansion of commercial printing.
Japan Printing was closely associated with Buddhist texts and later developed into illustrated and popular publications.
Korea Korea developed advanced printing techniques, including the use of movable metal type.

The growth of printing changed the relationship between knowledge and society. Texts that were once accessible mainly to elite groups gradually became available to wider sections.

3. Print Comes to Europe

Before the printing press, manuscripts were handwritten. Producing a manuscript required considerable time and skilled labour.

The demand for books increased with the growth of literacy, education, trade and urban society. European scholars and merchants increasingly required copies of books.

Manuscript Culture

  • Books copied by hand
  • Slow production
  • Expensive books
  • Limited number of copies
  • High dependence on scribes

Print Culture

  • Multiple copies
  • Faster production
  • Wider circulation
  • Growing readership
  • Rapid spread of ideas

4. Gutenberg and the Printing Press

Johann Gutenberg developed the first-known printing press in Europe around the 1430s–1440s.

Gutenberg adapted existing technologies and created a system of movable metal type that could be arranged, inked and pressed onto paper.

The Gutenberg Bible

One of the earliest major books printed by Gutenberg was the Bible. The printing of the Bible demonstrated the enormous potential of the new technology.

Why was Gutenberg important?
  • He transformed the production of books in Europe.
  • Printing became faster and more efficient.
  • Books could be produced in large quantities.
  • The European book market expanded.

5. The Print Revolution

The print revolution refers to the transformation brought about by the development and spread of printing technology.

Major Effects

  • Books became available in larger numbers.
  • Reading became increasingly widespread.
  • Ideas travelled faster.
  • Religious debates expanded.
  • Political criticism reached wider audiences.
  • Newspapers and journals developed.
  • New forms of literature became popular.
Key Concept: The print revolution was not merely a technological change. It transformed the circulation of knowledge, ideas and information.

6. Print and Religious Debates

Printing played a major role in religious debates in Europe.

Martin Luther

Martin Luther criticised certain practices and teachings of the Catholic Church. His writings were printed and circulated widely.

The rapid circulation of Luther's ideas contributed to the Protestant Reformation.

Important Relationship: Print → Wider circulation of religious ideas → Public debate → Challenge to established authority.

7. Print and Dissent

Printed material gave people new opportunities to question religious and political authorities.

However, rulers and religious authorities often feared the spread of criticism. They therefore attempted to control books and publications.

  • Print encouraged debate.
  • Print allowed dissenting voices to spread.
  • Authorities introduced censorship.
  • Controversial books could be banned.

8. Reading Mania

By the eighteenth century, literacy rates were increasing in many European societies. A growing reading public emerged.

Popular Printed Materials

  • Newspapers
  • Journals
  • Almanacs
  • Novels
  • Pamphlets
  • Political writings
  • Religious literature

Publishers attempted to reach different categories of readers by producing inexpensive and varied forms of printed material.

9. Print Culture and the French Revolution

Print culture helped create an intellectual environment in which new political ideas could circulate.

Major Ideas

  • Liberty
  • Equality
  • Reason
  • Criticism of absolute monarchy
  • Questioning of traditional authority
Thinker Associated Ideas
John Locke Government, rights and individual liberty.
Jean-Jacques Rousseau Social contract and popular sovereignty.
Voltaire Criticism of intolerance and traditional authority.
Important: Print culture alone did not cause the French Revolution. It helped circulate ideas and contributed to the intellectual environment in which revolutionary thinking developed.

10. Print Culture Comes to India

Printing technology reached India in the sixteenth century. Portuguese missionaries played an important role in the early development of printing in India.

Over time, printing expanded into Indian languages and became an important medium of religious, social and political communication.

Growth of Print in India

  • Religious texts were printed.
  • Newspapers and journals appeared.
  • Social reformers used print to express their ideas.
  • Political opinions were circulated.
  • Public debates expanded.

11. Religious Reform and Public Debates

During the nineteenth century, print became a powerful instrument for religious and social reform in India.

Different groups used newspapers, journals, pamphlets and books to defend their beliefs and challenge the views of opponents.

Important Idea: Print created a new public sphere in which religious and social questions could be discussed before a much wider audience.

12. Women and Print

Print culture opened new opportunities for women to access education and express their experiences.

Rashsundari Devi

Rashsundari Devi wrote Amar Jiban, an important autobiographical work. Her writing provides insight into the experiences of women in nineteenth-century India.

Tarabai Shinde

Tarabai Shinde strongly criticised gender inequality and social discrimination.

Pandita Ramabai

Pandita Ramabai wrote about the difficult conditions faced by upper-caste Hindu women and advocated social reform.

Board Point: Print enabled women not only to become readers but also to become writers and participants in public debates.

13. Print and the Poor

Cheap books and inexpensive printed material expanded access to reading among poorer sections of society.

  • Cheap editions made books more affordable.
  • Libraries increased access to reading material.
  • Workers and poor readers could engage with printed ideas.
  • Print helped spread social awareness.

14. Print and Workers

Workers used print to express their experiences and discuss social and economic issues.

Pamphlets, newspapers and other publications helped create communication networks among workers and reformers.

Print therefore became an important tool for social awareness and political discussion.

15. Print and Censorship

Colonial authorities were concerned about newspapers and publications that criticised British rule. As a result, laws were introduced to control the press.

Vernacular Press Act, 1878

The Vernacular Press Act was introduced by the colonial government in 1878 to control vernacular newspapers. It gave the government greater powers to restrict publications considered critical of colonial rule.

Remember: Vernacular Press Act → 1878 → Control over vernacular newspapers.
